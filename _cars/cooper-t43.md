## What it changed

On 19 January 1958, Stirling Moss won the Argentine Grand Prix in Rob Walker's Cooper-Climax T43. It was the first world championship grand prix win for a car with a [mid-engine layout]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#moteur-central-arriere), and Cooper's first. Goodwood adds two more firsts: for a privateer team, and for a car whose engine came from an outside supplier.

The T43 was not built for this race. Mark Hughes describes it as an adapted Formula 2 car, its engine enlarged to 2 litres. Doug Nye speaks of a 1.96-litre Cooper-Climax. With the Coventry Climax engine in the middle of the car, Goodwood says, it was better balanced than the front-engined cars, which helped in corners and with traction.

## Why Moss was driving it

Vanwall and BRM stayed away from Argentina: they had not had time to adapt their engines to the commercial fuel now required. Moss, offered a privately entered Maserati 250F, chose instead to ask Rob Walker to enter his Cooper. Walker agreed, but did not make the trip. The car was flown out, the entry having been settled very late.

## The race

- The distance was cut from 400 to 313 km, or 80 laps, because of the heat. Moss started seventh.
- In the early laps he was stuck in second gear, losing about fifteen seconds before the lever freed itself.
- The Cooper's wheels were held by four studs, Mark Hughes recalls: a tyre change would have taken two to three minutes, against less than a minute for the front-engined cars. The team chose not to stop. The tyres were only rated for 40 laps, according to Goodwood, and Moss finished on the canvas.
- Mechanic Alf Francis made a show of preparing tyres in the pits and counted down to a stop that never came. The bluff delayed Ferrari's response.
- Moss won by 2.7 seconds from Luigi Musso's Ferrari.

## Who designed it

None of the sources consulted credits the T43 to a named designer. [Owen Maddock]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/owen-maddock.md %}) was then chief designer of the Cooper Car Company. According to his Motor Sport obituary, his [bent-tube chassis]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#chassis-tubulaire) had become a standing joke, until Moss won in Argentina. [John Cooper]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-cooper.md %}) was the son of Charles Cooper, who founded the Cooper Car Company in 1948 according to Doug Nye. The engine was the work of Coventry Climax.

## Limitations

- **1957**: according to Goodwood, the T43 had been seen in action only five times in 1957. Its best result was fifth place for Roy Salvadori at Aintree; the Walker team ran it twice with Jack Brabham, retiring both times.
- It was soon replaced by the T45, with the engine set lower, new suspension and disc brakes.

## Legacy

- For Mark Hughes, the Argentine win "pointed the way to the future" for mid-engined cars. Cooper took the titles in 1959 and 1960.
- **A discrepancy over Monaco 1958.** Goodwood writes that the T43 won again at Monaco with Maurice Trintignant. The results published by GrandPrix.com list Trintignant's car as a Cooper-Climax **T45**; the only T43 in the field, Ron Flockhart's, failed to qualify. This site goes with the T45.
