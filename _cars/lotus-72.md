## What it changed

Classic Team Lotus sums up its radical features: radiators placed at the sides, wedge shape, inboard front brakes, torsion-bar suspension{% include src.html n="1" %}.

- **The radiators** left the nose for the flanks, in what are now called [sidepods]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#pontons){% include src.html n="2,3" %}. The low, wedge-shaped nose was freed up{% include src.html n="2" %}. The 1968 Lotus 56 turbine car had already shown a single-seater with no radiator at the front, but only because it did not need one{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **The front brakes** were moved inboard: discs and calipers no longer weighed on the wheel, which reduced unsprung mass{% include src.html n="2,3" %}.
- **The DFV bolted** to the rear carried the rear suspension, as on the [Lotus 49]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _cars/lotus-49.md %}) ([stressed engine]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#moteur-porteur)){% include src.html n="2" %}.

## Who designed it

Classic Team Lotus names [Maurice Philippe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/maurice-philippe.md %}) as chief designer, working with [Colin Chapman]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/colin-chapman.md %}){% include src.html n="1" %}; Forix describes the car as drawn by Philippe under Chapman's supervision{% include src.html n="3" %}.

## Limitations

- The debut was a failure{% include src.html n="2,3" %}. The car was reworked: the anti-dive and anti-squat geometries were removed, as they cut the drivers off from the feel of the car{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- At Monza, in 1970, Jochen Rindt was killed in practice{% include src.html n="2,3" %}. According to the Revs Institute, the accident was caused by the failure of the right-front brake shaft{% include src.html n="2" %}. Rindt became world champion posthumously{% include src.html n="1,2,4" %}.
- By 1975 the car had aged: too much weight at the rear, and front tyres that did not get warm enough, according to Forix{% include src.html n="3" %}.

## Legacy

- The 72 raced for six consecutive championship seasons{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- Its number of victories depends on the source: **20 Grands Prix** according to Lotus Cars, Forix and the Revs Institute{% include src.html n="2,3,4" %}, 22 according to Classic Team Lotus{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- Drivers' titles for Rindt in 1970 and Emerson Fittipaldi in 1972; three constructors' titles{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
- According to the Revs Institute, it set the layout of a racing single-seater still in use today{% include src.html n="2" %}.
