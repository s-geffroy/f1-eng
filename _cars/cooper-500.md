## What it changed

This is not an F1 car but a small 500cc single-seater built in 1946. Its engine, a motorcycle single, sits in the middle of the car, "for largely practical reasons", as the 500 Owners Association puts it. This is the layout Cooper would carry all the way to F1, as the [mid-engine layout]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#moteur-central-arriere).

The same source points out that the layout was not new. What made the car good was the combination with light weight, a low [polar moment of inertia]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#moment-polaire) and all-independent suspension: its handling was excellent.

[John Cooper]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-cooper.md %}) himself declined to take credit. He told Nigel Roebuck that with a motorcycle engine and chain drive, putting the engine in the back of a 500 was "the only logical thing to do". A 500, he said, was "a four-wheeled motorcycle — or a baby Auto Union, if you like".

## Who designed it

John Cooper and his childhood friend Eric Brandon built two cars, one each. Charles Cooper, John's father and a trained mechanic, ran a garage in Surbiton; he backed the project and, according to the 500 Owners Association, obtained the engine through his motorcycle contacts. The aluminium body was made by a specialist, Charlie Robinson. Brandon's car, slightly longer, had revisions that appeared on later production Coopers.

Work on the first car began in June 1946 and took about five weeks.

## How it was built

- **Engine and drivetrain**: an air-cooled 500cc JAP single from speedway racing. A chain ran to a Triumph Speed Twin gearbox, and a second chain to the rear axle.
- **Chassis**: a ladder frame, with hoops to support the body and stiffen the structure.
- **Suspension**: independent at both ends, using Fiat 500 Topolino parts, with lower wishbones and a transverse leaf spring; the wheels and steering also came from the Fiat. The two sources describe the borrowing slightly differently: the 500 Owners Association speaks of a crashed Topolino whose front arrangement was reused at the rear, Doug Nye of two front ends from scrapped Topolinos welded back to back.

## Limitations

The engine and gearbox mounts could not cope with the torque and vibration of the single. John Cooper, who also over-revved the engine, bent a valve.

## Legacy

- **July 1946**: debut at the Prescott hill climb.
- **1947**: in May, at the White Hart meeting, John Cooper finished second and Brandon third; in June, Brandon won at Prescott; in July, he won the first post-war race at Gransden Lodge. The first prototype was sold at the end of the year.
- The Cooper Car Company came afterwards, in 1948 according to Doug Nye. The prototype's layout led to the Cooper F1 cars that won in 1958 and took the titles in 1959 and 1960.
