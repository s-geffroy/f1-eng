## What it changed

The Lotus 98T is the car with which [pneumatic valve return]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#rappel-pneumatique) reached racing. On Renault's EF15 B [turbo]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#turbocompresseur) V6, the steel springs that close the valves were replaced by compressed gas. According to Renault, the engine brought two major innovations from the start of the 1986 season: static ignition and, "above all", pneumatic valve return, "a pioneering revs-boost solution" that would appear on all F1 engines in the 1990s. Motor Sport adds that each spark plug had its own miniature ignition coil.

The gain was measured in revs. According to Nigel Roebuck (Autosport), the development work gave the drivers "another thousand revs or so". Jean Sage of Renault Sport reported what the drivers said: they no longer had to change up in awkward places, "like in the middle of a corner". Motor Sport quotes 12,500rpm and a reliable 900bhp or so in race trim, and well over 1,000bhp in qualifying; the magazine adds that these figures are speculative.

**Sources do not date the debut the same way.** Renault has the engine in service from the start of the season. At the Brazilian Grand Prix, the opening round, Autosport writes that Senna was "using the only valve-springless engine in the race", with "no real power advantage over the regular engines". But Jean Sage qualified the idea of a Rio debut: "Actually, Patrick Tambay ran one at Monza, but he never knew it." According to him, work on the engine had started in 1984, in great secrecy.

## How it was built

- **Chassis**: according to Motor Sport, a one-piece moulding of [carbon fibre]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#composite-carbone) and aluminium, with bulkheads machined from solid aluminium. Only four chassis were built for the 1986 season.
- **Engine**: Renault's 1.5-litre turbo V6, in EF15 B form, then EF15 C from mid-1986 according to Renault, with two Garrett turbochargers.
- **A scarce engine.** At the Spanish Grand Prix, the second round, Renault had qualifying engines of this type for all its customers, but race engines were still in short supply. "We have only three here," said Jean Sage. "One for Senna, one for Senna's spare car, and one for Ligier." According to Roebuck, Martin Brundle's Tyrrell was not yet running the latest "pneumatic" engine.

## Who designed it

According to Motor Sport, the car was designed by [Gérard Ducarouge]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gerard-ducarouge.md %}) and [Martin Ogilvie]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/martin-ogilvie.md %}). The source does not say how the work was shared; Ducarouge's entry notes that Motor Sport sees him as the technical leader rather than the draughtsman of the cars credited to him.

The engine was Renault Sport's work. Motor Sport credits the EF15 B, an evolution of the 1985 unit, to chief engineer [Bernard Dudot]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/bernard-dudot.md %}); Roebuck also refers to Dudot's "development work". The idea of pneumatic valve return was older: in July 1995, GrandPrix.com wrote that Renault had pioneered it "back in the early 1980s", "the idea having been dreamed up by [Jean-Pierre Boudy]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/jean-pierre-boudy.md %})", who was replaced at the head of research and development in 1984. The idea, its development and its arrival in racing therefore belong to different people, within the same team.

## Limitations

- **No extra power at first.** In Brazil, according to Autosport, the springless engine brought no real power advantage; the Renault cars lacked power, or rather fuel, against the Hondas and TAGs, and Senna was slower than the leaders on the straight.
- **The engine shortage.** In April, Renault still had only three race engines of this type at the track, shared between Lotus and Ligier.
- **The championship.** Despite eight pole positions according to Renault, Senna won only two races and finished fourth in the championship, behind the Williams-Hondas of Mansell and Piquet and Prost's McLaren.

## Legacy

- **1986**: in Brazil, Senna started from pole and finished second, 34.8 seconds behind Nelson Piquet (Williams-Honda), according to Autosport. He then won the Spanish Grand Prix at Jerez, by 14 thousandths of a second from Nigel Mansell, and the United States Grand Prix in Detroit. He was fourth in the championship with 55 points; Lotus were third among constructors, with 58 points. According to Renault, the EF15 B/C engine scored two wins, eight pole positions and eight podiums.
- **Chassis 98T-3**, winner in Spain and Detroit, scored two wins, five pole positions and three further podiums in eight races, according to Motor Sport.
- **The end of an era.** Renault then suspended its F1 involvement; for Renault, the EF15 closed the turbo era. Pneumatic valve return lived on: according to Renault, it was fitted to every F1 engine from the 1990s.
