## What it changed

The Tyrrell P34 is the only six-wheeled car ever to start a Grand Prix: four small steered wheels at the front, two normal-sized wheels at the rear{% include src.html n="2,5" %}.

When it was unveiled, people assumed the aim was a smaller frontal area{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. That was not the point, Motor Sport explains: frontal area was set by the huge rear tyres{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. According to Motor Sport, the reason was [downforce]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#appui){% include src.html n="2" %}. An exposed wheel in the airflow generates [lift]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#portance), and the bigger the wheel, the greater the lift{% include src.html n="2" %}. Smaller wheels, twice as many of them, keep the rubber and braking area while generating less lift{% include src.html n="2" %}. Other teams countered the lift of their front tyres with more front wing; the Tyrrell could do without, and gain on the straights{% include src.html n="2" %}. Derek Gardner had put a figure on it: the equivalent of "40-odd horsepower"{% include src.html n="1" %}.

The context explains the approach. Almost the whole field had the same engine, the same gearbox and the same tyres{% include src.html n="2" %}. "We needed an unfair advantage," said Gardner, "and the six-wheeler was it."{% include src.html n="2" %}

Goodyear agreed to make the tiny front tyres{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Gardner wanted nine-inch; they compromised on ten{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Who designed it

[Derek Gardner]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/derek-gardner.md %}), with Ken Tyrrell's approval{% include src.html n="1" %}. The idea dated back to 1968 and a quite different purpose{% include src.html n="2" %}. Gardner was then working on the four-wheel drive of the Lotus turbine car at Indianapolis, and later of the Matra F1 car; spreading the load over four front wheels, two of them driven, was meant to steady those cars{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. He proposed a six-wheeler to Andy Granatelli, the head of STP, to no avail{% include src.html n="1,2,6" %}. He revived the project at Tyrrell, with a different objective{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.

The first prototype used the rear end of the 1975 Tyrrell 007{% include src.html n="1" %}. Gardner left the team during the 1977 season, right after Monza by his own account{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. [Maurice Philippe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/maurice-philippe.md %}) then widened the front track and moved the oil radiators forward to recover the lost front-end grip; according to Motor Sport, it did not work{% include src.html n="2" %}. Gardner had hired him in mid-summer 1977 to fit the Renault turbo [V6]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#architecture-moteur) into the six-wheeler; when Gardner left, there was a mock-up engine and a car in an advanced state{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Limitations

- **The front tyres.** The small Goodyears were used by one team only{% include src.html n="1" %}. Goodyear developed the rears and the normal fronts, and the P34's fronts stood still: "the advantage of the six-wheel concept was going rapidly out of the window," said Gardner{% include src.html n="1" %}. Their cross-ply construction was another problem: stiffening the carcass also stiffened the sidewall, and under braking the tyre collapsed onto the rim{% include src.html n="1" %}. Michelin, when approached, was keen to develop a ten-inch radial, but by the time it was ready Goodyear would have moved further ahead{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **The brakes.** The brake fluid overheated inside the small wheels{% include src.html n="1" %}. At the first race, Depailler went off for that reason{% include src.html n="1" %}. Braking also had to be balanced across four front wheels{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **1977.** The enveloping glassfibre bodywork was heavy, until Kevlar arrived later in the year{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **A sceptical driver.** Jody Scheckter, who won with it, called it "a rubbish car"{% include src.html n="2,5" %}. He said he never agreed with what he saw as its two fundamental concepts, reducing frontal area and improving braking; his reading differs from Gardner's{% include src.html n="2" %}.

## Legacy

- **1976**: debut at the Spanish Grand Prix at Jarama, the fourth round; Patrick Depailler qualified behind only Hunt and Lauda{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. On 13 June at Anderstorp, Scheckter took the car's only pole and won ahead of Depailler{% include src.html n="2,5" %}. According to Motor Sport, one or other driver reached the podium ten times in thirteen races{% include src.html n="2" %}. Scheckter finished third in the championship, Depailler fourth, and Tyrrell third among constructors, with 71 points{% include src.html n="1,2,3" %}.
- **1977**: one second place and three thirds{% include src.html n="2" %}. Depailler and Ronnie Peterson finished ninth and fourteenth{% include src.html n="1" %}. In testing at Silverstone, Karl Kempf, the Goodyear engineer tasked with fitting sensors to the company's cars, went through the P34's data with Derek Gardner{% include src.html n="4" %}; in his words, even detailed measurements "couldn't save it"{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- In 1978 the Tyrrell 008 went back to four wheels{% include src.html n="2,5" %}; a four-wheel maximum rule later banned six-wheelers, in 1982 or 1983 depending on the source{% include src.html n="5,7" %}.
