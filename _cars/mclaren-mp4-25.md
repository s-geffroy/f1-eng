## What it changed

The MP4-25 introduced what the paddock called the "F-duct", after the letter of the Vodafone logo next to its inlet; inside McLaren it carried the project number RW80{% include src.html n="1" %}. The principle: on the straights, the driver stalled the rear [wing]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#aileron), cutting downforce and drag{% include src.html n="1" %}.

According to Autosport, a snorkel on top of the chassis fed a duct that opened into the cockpit{% include src.html n="1" %}. When the driver covered the hole, first with his knee and, on a later version, with his elbow, the air was sent to a fluidic switch{% include src.html n="1" %}. The switch diverted air from the airbox to the rear wing, where it came out through a slot: the flow over the suction surface broke away, and the wing stalled{% include src.html n="1" %}.

The rest of the car answered the new rules. In-race [refuelling]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#ravitaillement) was banned in 2010: twice as much fuel had to be carried, and the car grew longer{% include src.html n="4" %}. According to Tim Goss, its chief engineer, the philosophy was to get the absolute maximum out of the [diffuser]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#diffuseur): the gearbox was stretched to push the engine forward, and louvres at the back of the duct actually opened up the floor{% include src.html n="3" %}. Goss reckoned that around half the teams later copied some solution along those lines{% include src.html n="3" %}.

## Who designed it

A team. GrandPrix.com calls [Paddy Lowe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/paddy-lowe.md %}) McLaren's technical director, the press pack its engineering director, alongside Tim Goss, "Chief Engineer, MP4-25"{% include src.html n="3,4" %}. According to him, the [F-duct]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#f-duct) was developed from the end of 2008; McLaren had considered running it in 2009{% include src.html n="3" %}.

Chief engineer Tim Goss and chief aerodynamicist Doug McKiernan collected the Pioneering and Innovation Award at the 2010 Autosport Awards for the F-duct{% include src.html n="2" %}. Both insisted that the credit be shared across the whole company, because of the leap of faith needed to get it approved{% include src.html n="2" %}. "Aerodynamicists will often come up with an idea that seems pretty stupid," said Goss, "and when you say 'we are going to use the driver to somehow stall the rear wing' then you will have lots of doubters."{% include src.html n="2" %} The governing body also had to be convinced, since the rules prohibited driver-operated aerodynamics{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Goss gave it credit: it "heard the arguments, believed them and let us get on with it"{% include src.html n="2" %}.

## Limitations

- **Copied, then banned.** Sauber raced its own version from the second round, in Australia; Mercedes, Ferrari, Red Bull, Force India, Williams and Renault followed during the season{% include src.html n="1" %}. At a meeting at the 2010 Spanish Grand Prix it was voted out for 2011, on cost and safety grounds{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **An uneven car.** According to Autosport, the F-duct could not make up for the car's deficiencies{% include src.html n="1" %}. Goss put its poor behaviour over bumps down to its aerodynamic characteristics{% include src.html n="3" %}. McLaren also admitted being slow on the [exhaust-blown diffuser]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#diffuseur-souffle), introduced a race after Ferrari and Renault{% include src.html n="3" %}.

## Legacy

- **2010**: five wins, shared between Lewis Hamilton and Jenson Button{% include src.html n="1" %}. McLaren finished second among constructors, with 454 points to Red Bull's 498{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- The F-duct was replaced in 2011 by hydraulically operated [DRS]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#drs){% include src.html n="1" %}.
- In 2012 Mercedes raced a variation of it, linked to its DRS{% include src.html n="1" %}. [Ross Brawn]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ross-brawn.md %}) argued at the time that it could not be illegal, since F-ducts had never been properly defined{% include src.html n="1" %}.
