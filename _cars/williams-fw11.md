## What it changed

For 1986, the fuel allowed in a race was cut from 220 to 195 litres{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. According to Mark Hughes, the FW11 was designed around this smaller tank; it was lower than the FW10, which had won the last three races of 1985{% include src.html n="1" %}. At Honda, Denis Jenkinson writes, development focused on fuel consumption and electronic engine management{% include src.html n="3" %}.

It was also one of the first F1 cars whose engineers could follow it remotely{% include src.html n="2,5" %}. In January 1987 Motor Sport described a pit-to-car radio and a telemetry system that let engineers watch the instrument read-outs during the race{% include src.html n="2" %}. Electronics also controlled boost, ignition and fuel injection; the driver had four cockpit-adjustable [turbo]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#turbocompresseur) boost settings{% include src.html n="2" %}.

**The sources do not describe this system the same way.** Mark Hughes speaks of a "rudimentary" telemetry system downloading information to the garage at the end of each lap{% include src.html n="1" %}. Honda describes a data logger developed from mid-1985 by Toshihiro Ito's team, which later became telemetry{% include src.html n="5" %}; on its RA166E page, the manufacturer even states that data were transmitted in real time as early as 1986{% include src.html n="4" %}, and that Williams-Honda became the first F1 team to put such a system into practical use in 1986{% include src.html n="5" %}. No independent source confirms that claim. This site therefore speaks of on-board [data acquisition]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#acquisition-de-donnees), passed to the pits in a form that remains disputed.

## How it was built

- **Chassis**: according to Mark Hughes, an inverted-U tub with carbon fibre bulkheads rather than aluminium, built in-house at Didcot and bonded to a carbon fibre floor{% include src.html n="1" %}. Motor Sport speaks of a moulded carbon/Kevlar/aluminium [composite]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#composite-carbone), designed with a General Electric Calma [CAD/CAM]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#cao) system installed at Didcot in late 1985{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Engine**: Honda's 1.5-litre turbo [V6]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#architecture-moteur), over 1100bhp in qualifying and over 900bhp in the race according to Mark Hughes{% include src.html n="1" %}. Honda calls it the RA166E{% include src.html n="4" %}; Motor Sport, in 1987, refers to an "F" version of the 80-degree RA163 V6{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Transmission and brakes**: a six-speed gearbox, mounted behind the rear axle line{% include src.html n="1" %}. Carbon brake discs from SEP according to Motor Sport{% include src.html n="2" %}; Mark Hughes speaks of cast-iron discs{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Suspension**: double wishbones all round, with inboard spring/damper units operated by pushrods according to Motor Sport{% include src.html n="2" %}; Mark Hughes indicates pullrods at the rear{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Aerodynamics**: radiators and intercoolers arranged symmetrically in the [sidepods]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#pontons){% include src.html n="2" %}; according to Mark Hughes, flat wing profiles, no bargeboards and no sidepod undercuts, but a slight "coke bottle" taper at the rear{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Who designed it

According to Mark Hughes, [Patrick Head]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/patrick-head.md %}), the technical director, had overall responsibility and conceived the car with chief designer [Frank Dernie]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/frank-dernie.md %}){% include src.html n="1" %}. Frank Williams's pre-season road accident also left Head effectively running the team{% include src.html n="1,3" %}. Jenkinson notes that at the races Dernie looked after Nelson Piquet's car{% include src.html n="3" %}. The engine and its electronics were Honda's work{% include src.html n="3,5" %}.

## Limitations

The two drivers took points off each other{% include src.html n="1" %}. According to Mark Hughes, that was the main reason they lost the drivers' title to Alain Prost{% include src.html n="1" %}. Jenkinson instead blames Nigel Mansell's defeat on a "rogue" Goodyear tyre in the final race{% include src.html n="3" %}.

## Legacy

- **1986**: nine wins from sixteen races, five for Mansell and four for Piquet{% include src.html n="2,7" %}. Williams won the constructors' championship with 141 points{% include src.html n="6" %}; it was Honda's first constructors' title{% include src.html n="1,4" %}. Prost was champion with 72 points, ahead of Mansell (70) and Piquet (69){% include src.html n="1,8" %}.
- The FW11B, its evolution, took Nelson Piquet to the title in 1987{% include src.html n="1" %}.
