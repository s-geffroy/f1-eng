## What it changed

The MP4-24 was McLaren's car for the first season of [KERS]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#kers), allowed from 2009{% include src.html n="2,5" %}. The system recovered energy under braking, stored it for a lap and released it when the driver pressed a button on the steering wheel{% include src.html n="2" %}. The rules capped the boost at 80bhp for 6.6 seconds per lap according to formula1.com{% include src.html n="5" %}; McLaren's press pack gives 400 kJ and 6.7 seconds{% include src.html n="2" %}. McLaren expected 0.3 to 0.5 seconds per lap from it{% include src.html n="2" %}.

The system was developed with Mercedes-Benz HighPerformanceEngines, which had been working on it for almost two years when the car was launched{% include src.html n="2" %}. According to Crash.net, the company Zytek worked closely on it{% include src.html n="3" %}. [Andy Cowell]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/andy-cowell.md %}), head of Mercedes High Performance Powertrains in 2019, traced the start of "serious electric propulsion" at Mercedes back to that 2007 decision to build its own KERS, with investment at Brixworth in motors, electronics and software{% include src.html n="4" %}.

KERS came at a price. "It weighs a lot and that reduces your freedom to use ballast to play with the car's configuration," said Paddy Lowe; it "did affect us quite badly at the beginning"{% include src.html n="1" %}. formula1.com recalls that the 2009 systems were heavy and complex to package{% include src.html n="5" %}; according to Autosport, only four teams raced one, and two dropped it before the end of the year{% include src.html n="4" %}.

## Who designed it

McLaren's technical team, with [Paddy Lowe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/paddy-lowe.md %}) as engineering director and Pat Fry as chief engineer of the MP4-24{% include src.html n="1" %}. KERS was a joint effort by McLaren and Mercedes{% include src.html n="2" %}.

An ironic detail: the 2009 aerodynamic rules that hampered the car so much came out of an Overtaking Working Group that included Lowe, [Rory Byrne]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/rory-byrne.md %}) and [Pat Symonds]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/pat-symonds.md %}){% include src.html n="2,5" %}.

## Limitations

- **A huge deficit.** At the winter test in Barcelona, with its latest upgrades fitted, the car was still 2.4 seconds off, Lowe recalled{% include src.html n="1" %}. According to Fry, there was no fundamental mistake: "We hadn't bolted enough downforce onto it."{% include src.html n="1" %}
- **The double diffuser.** Brawn, Toyota and Williams raced a double [diffuser]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#diffuseur) from the start, declared legal on appeal on 14 April{% include src.html n="1" %}. According to Pat Fry, McLaren had looked at something similar around Christmas 2007, but lacked the resources to run two programmes at once; Lowe pointed out that the team had a single [wind tunnel]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#soufflerie), where Honda had four on its 2009 project, by his account{% include src.html n="1" %}. Ross Brawn, for his part, speaks of three wind tunnels working in parallel at one stage{% include src.html n="7" %}. It took McLaren six to eight weeks to learn how to use the double diffuser{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Legacy

- **The turnaround.** The T3 floor, the first designed from the outset to exploit the double-diffuser loophole, arrived in Germany with a new front wing: worth about eight tenths according to Fry{% include src.html n="1" %}. Seventeen versions of the floor came and went during the season{% include src.html n="1" %}. Lowe judged the 2.8-second gain credited to the car over the year roughly right{% include src.html n="1" %}. This turnaround came despite the in-season testing ban; Jonathan Neale praised test driver Pedro de la Rosa's work "in the simulator"{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **2009**: Lewis Hamilton won in Hungary, passing Mark Webber with KERS, which he said played "a big part"{% include src.html n="4" %}: it was the first win for a car fitted with such a system{% include src.html n="3,4" %}. He then won in Singapore{% include src.html n="1" %}. In Abu Dhabi he took pole by more than six tenths, four of them attributed to KERS{% include src.html n="1" %}. McLaren finished third among constructors, with 71 points{% include src.html n="6" %}.
- KERS disappeared in 2010{% include src.html n="1" %}. According to Autosport, it remained for Mercedes the foundation of its 2014 hybrid power unit{% include src.html n="4" %}.
