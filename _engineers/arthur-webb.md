## What he did

Arthur Webb is a British aeronautical engineer and a specialist in [carbon composite]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#composite-carbone) structures. Coming from aviation, he gave [John Barnard]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-barnard.md %}) the materials and stress calculations he needed for the carbon [monocoque]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#monocoque) of the 1981 McLaren MP4/1.

## In aviation

- **Education and early career.** Trained in aeronautical engineering at Queen Mary, he stressed the wings of the Armstrong Whitworth Argosy, an aircraft that made early use of bonded composites. During the "brain drain", he first worked on the wings of the Lockheed C-5 Galaxy for a British subcontractor, then moved to the United States in the 1960s.
- **Carbon in civil aviation.** Tasked with finding a practical use for the carbon fibre developed at the Royal Aircraft Establishment, he led the team that designed the first certified civil aircraft component made of carbon: load-carrying rudder panels for the VC10.
- **Rolls-Royce.** He recounts persuading Rolls-Royce to adopt carbon fibre for structural applications.
- **Concorde.** His final years at BAC, according to the Royal Aeronautical Society, included Concorde and the difficult period leading to the nationalisation of Britain's aircraft industry.
- **ML Aviation and Marconi Space.** After leaving the nationalised BAC at Weybridge, he joined the small special projects office at ML Aviation, then Marconi Space: drones, the Tornado's JP233 anti-runway bomb, the Skynet 4 satellite, the European Space Agency's Eureka project.

## At McLaren, then Ferrari

- **The MP4/1 (1981), according to Barnard.** Barnard told Motor Sport that he was invited to British Aerospace at Weybridge, where the outer cowling of the Rolls-Royce RB211 engine was being made as a carbon fibre and aluminium [honeycomb]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#nid-d-abeille) sandwich. There he met Webb, an "old school" engineer from the days of the slide rule but with "a tremendous knowledge of carbon"; out of their talks came the conclusion that carbon had the properties he was after.
- **The MP4/1, according to Autosport and the Royal Aeronautical Society.** According to Autosport, initial contacts with British Aerospace at Weybridge led to an introduction: Webb combined the materials design expertise Barnard needed with an old-school, slide-rule approach that Barnard valued. The Royal Aeronautical Society adds that he worked with Barnard as an informal consultant on the first carbon chassis of an F1 car, improving both performance and driver safety.
- **Ferrari.** When Barnard considered joining Ferrari, he wanted Webb for the carbon fibre work and the stressing. The team then had about a year to set up the Guildford Technical Office before the car had to reach the grid, and Webb also had to win Enzo Ferrari's approval.
- **After Ferrari.** He became an independent engineering consultant, on projects ranging from trolleybuses in post-Cold War Hungary to MRI scanner beds and defence work. One of his clients, St Bernard's Composites, made him technical director with a seat on the board; the company then worked with Rolls-Royce, Airbus, Martin-Baker and Imperial College.
- **Elsewhere.** He also worked to bring carbon fibre into rowing.

## Attribution

Autosport presents the MP4/1 as Barnard's car, and the move to carbon as his choice; in Barnard's own account, his talks with Webb reinforced a choice he was already considering. Webb brought materials science and structural calculation; he was not its designer.

## Source discrepancies

- **His employer**: the Royal Aeronautical Society places him at BAC, at Weybridge, and notes its nationalisation; Autosport and Barnard speak of British Aerospace at Weybridge. The place matches; the company name differs.
- **Chronology**: the interview summaries date neither his departure from BAC nor his spells at ML Aviation and Marconi Space; this site cannot place them relative to the MP4/1.
- **The nature of the sources**: most of the above comes from summaries of an oral history interview Webb himself gave in 2024 and 2025 for the Royal Aeronautical Society and the National Aerospace Library. This site has not listened to the recordings; it sticks to the published summaries.

## A short entry

Webb is known mainly through his own testimony and through what Barnard and the history of the MP4/1 say of him. This site sticks to those facts.
