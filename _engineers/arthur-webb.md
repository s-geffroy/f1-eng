## What he did

Arthur Webb is a British aeronautical engineer and a specialist in [carbon composite]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#composite-carbone) structures{% include src.html n="1,8" %}. Coming from aviation, he gave [John Barnard]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-barnard.md %}) the materials and stress calculations he needed for the carbon [monocoque]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#monocoque) of the 1981 McLaren MP4/1{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.

## In aviation

- **Education and early career.** Trained in aeronautical engineering at Queen Mary, he stressed the wings of the Armstrong Whitworth Argosy, an aircraft that made early use of bonded composites{% include src.html n="5" %}. During the "brain drain", he first worked on the wings of the Lockheed C-5 Galaxy for a British subcontractor, then moved to the United States in the 1960s{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- **Carbon in civil aviation.** Tasked with finding a practical use for the carbon fibre developed at the Royal Aircraft Establishment, he led the team that designed the first certified civil aircraft component made of carbon: load-carrying rudder panels for the VC10{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Rolls-Royce.** He recounts persuading Rolls-Royce to adopt carbon fibre for structural applications{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Concorde.** His final years at BAC, according to the Royal Aeronautical Society, included Concorde and the difficult period leading to the nationalisation of Britain's aircraft industry{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **ML Aviation and Marconi Space.** After leaving the nationalised BAC at Weybridge, he joined the small special projects office at ML Aviation, then Marconi Space: drones, the Tornado's JP233 anti-runway bomb, the Skynet 4 satellite, the European Space Agency's Eureka project{% include src.html n="6" %}.

## At McLaren, then Ferrari

- **The MP4/1 (1981), according to Barnard.** Barnard told Motor Sport that he was invited to British Aerospace at Weybridge, where the outer cowling of the Rolls-Royce RB211 engine was being made as a carbon fibre and aluminium [honeycomb]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#nid-d-abeille) sandwich{% include src.html n="8" %}. There he met Webb, an "old school" engineer from the days of the slide rule but with "a tremendous knowledge of carbon"; out of their talks came the conclusion that carbon had the properties he was after{% include src.html n="8" %}.
- **The MP4/1, according to Autosport and the Royal Aeronautical Society.** According to Autosport, initial contacts with British Aerospace at Weybridge led to an introduction: Webb combined the materials design expertise Barnard needed with an old-school, slide-rule approach that Barnard valued{% include src.html n="1" %}. The Royal Aeronautical Society adds that he worked with Barnard as an informal consultant on the first carbon chassis of an F1 car, improving both performance and driver safety{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Ferrari.** When Barnard considered joining Ferrari, he wanted Webb for the carbon fibre work and the stressing{% include src.html n="4" %}. The team then had about a year to set up the Guildford Technical Office before the car had to reach the grid, and Webb also had to win Enzo Ferrari's approval{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- **After Ferrari.** He became an independent engineering consultant, on projects ranging from trolleybuses in post-Cold War Hungary to MRI scanner beds and defence work{% include src.html n="7" %}. One of his clients, St Bernard's Composites, made him technical director with a seat on the board; the company then worked with Rolls-Royce, Airbus, Martin-Baker and Imperial College{% include src.html n="7" %}.
- **Elsewhere.** He also worked to bring carbon fibre into rowing{% include src.html n="2" %}.

## Attribution

Autosport presents the MP4/1 as Barnard's car, and the move to carbon as his choice{% include src.html n="1" %}; in Barnard's own account, his talks with Webb reinforced a choice he was already considering{% include src.html n="8" %}. Webb brought materials science and structural calculation; he was not the car's designer{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Where sources disagree

- **His employer**: the Royal Aeronautical Society places him at BAC, at Weybridge, and notes its nationalisation{% include src.html n="2,3,6" %}; Autosport and Barnard speak of British Aerospace at Weybridge{% include src.html n="1,8" %}. The place matches; the company name differs.
- **Chronology**: the interview summaries date neither his departure from BAC nor his spells at ML Aviation and Marconi Space; this site cannot place them relative to the MP4/1.
- **The nature of the sources**: most of the above comes from summaries of an oral history interview Webb himself gave in 2024 and 2025 for the Royal Aeronautical Society and the National Aerospace Library{% include src.html n="4,7" %}. This site has not listened to the recordings; it sticks to the published summaries.

## Limits of the sources

Webb is known mainly through his own testimony and through what Barnard and the history of the MP4/1 say of him. This site sticks to those facts.
