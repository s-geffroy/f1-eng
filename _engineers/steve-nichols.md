## What he did

formula1.com attributes the creation of the **McLaren MP4/4**, which it presents as the most dominant F1 car in history, sometimes to Steve Nichols, sometimes to [Gordon Murray]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gordon-murray.md %}).

## Technical career

- **Hercules.** Before motor racing, he spent four years at Hercules working on [carbon composites]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#composite-carbone), then a new material. According to [John Barnard]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-barnard.md %}), it was Nichols who later put him in touch with his former colleagues, who would make the carbon tub of the MP4/1.
- **McLaren.** In 1980, Barnard hired him at Project 4 to work with him on the MP4/1 chassis. The MP4/3 is the first McLaren credited to his technical direction; in 1987, he led the design of the MP4/4, whose authorship is disputed.
- **Ferrari.** There he replaced Barnard as head of chassis design, and produced the F92A, a failure, with [Jean-Claude Migeot]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/jean-claude-migeot.md %}).
- **Jaguar.** In late 2000, he became technical director of Jaguar Racing.

## The MP4/4: disputed authorship

Nichols himself presents the MP4/4 as an evolution of the MP4/3, not as a "low-line" revolution. [Gordon Murray]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gordon-murray.md %}) disputes this account, and Autosport also names [Bob Bell]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/bob-bell.md %}), [David North]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/david-north.md %}), [Matthew Jeffreys]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/matthew-jeffreys.md %}) and Steve Nielsen (uncertain attribution: according to GrandPrix.com, the well-known Steve Nielsen was then working at Team Lotus) among its authors. His arrival at Ferrari is dated January 1989 by GrandPrix.com, 1990 by Motor Sport, a date consistent with Nichols's account of being approached by Prost in Adelaide at the end of 1989.
