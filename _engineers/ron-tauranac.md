## What he did

Ron Tauranac designed the Brabhams of the 1960s, including the cars of the 1966 and 1967 world titles, then the Ralts that dominated the junior formulas. His cars had a reputation for solidly engineered simplicity.

## Technical career

- **Australia.** From 1950, with his brother Austin, he built hillclimb cars called Ralt, after their initials (Ron and Austin Lewis Tauranac). Jack Brabham, who had gone to race for Cooper, wrote to him for ideas on improving his Coopers.
- **Motor Racing Developments.** He joined Brabham in England in 1960. Their company, Motor Racing Developments, produced a Formula Junior of his design in 1961, which became the Brabham BT1: "BT", for Brabham and Tauranac.
- **First F1 cars.** The BT3 made its debut at the 1962 German Grand Prix. Dan Gurney gave the marque its first world championship win, in France in 1964, with the BT7.
- **The titles.** For the 1966 3-litre formula, he drew relatively simple Repco V8-engined cars, the BT19, BT20 and BT24: Jack Brabham was world champion in 1966, Denny Hulme in 1967.
- **Production constructor.** Brabhams were also customer cars: 79 F3 and 12 F2 cars left the factory in 1965.
- **BT34 (1971).** After Brabham retired, he drew the "lobster claw" BT34, with twin radiators ahead of the front wheels.
- **Ralt.** After selling the team to Bernie Ecclestone, he founded Ralt. The RT1 won the first European F3 championship, in 1975, with Larry Perkins; the [ground-effect]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#effet-de-sol) RT3 followed it.
- **Honda.** His relationship with Honda began with the 1966 Brabham-Honda F2 cars. According to The Race, it was he who put Honda in touch with Frank Williams and [Patrick Head]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/patrick-head.md %}).

## The choice of the spaceframe

- **Eight years without a monocoque.** Tauranac stayed with the [spaceframe chassis]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#chassis-tubulaire) for eight years after the [monocoque]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#monocoque) appeared in F1. In his view, the frame allowed glassfibre panels to be shaped more sympathetically to the aerodynamics than the boxy contours a tub then imposed.
- **The BT33 (1970).** His first monocoque, for Jack Brabham's last season, won first time out, in South Africa. According to The Race, it was the new regulations on fuel tanks that forced this construction method on him.

## Where sources disagree

- **Taking over the team.** According to the BRDC, Brabham sold him his shareholding at the end of 1969; according to The Race and Goodwood, Tauranac took over the team when Brabham stopped racing, at the end of 1970.
- **His departure.** The Race dates the sale to Ecclestone to the end of 1971. Goodwood adds that he stayed on for a time to design the cars, until Ecclestone reorganised the team without him; the BRDC says he left within a year of the sale, on terms he soon came to regret.
