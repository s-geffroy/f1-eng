## What he did

Ron Tauranac designed the Brabhams of the 1960s, including the cars of the 1966 and 1967 world titles, then the Ralts that dominated the junior formulas{% include src.html n="1" %}. His cars had a reputation for solidly engineered simplicity{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Technical career

- **Australia.** From 1950, with his brother Austin, he built hillclimb cars called Ralt, after their initials (Ron and Austin Lewis Tauranac){% include src.html n="2,3" %}. Jack Brabham, who had gone to race for Cooper, wrote to him for ideas on improving his Coopers{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Motor Racing Developments.** He joined Brabham in England in 1960{% include src.html n="2" %}. Their company, Motor Racing Developments, produced a Formula Junior of his design in 1961, which became the Brabham BT1: "BT", for Brabham and Tauranac{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **First F1 cars.** The BT3 made its debut at the 1962 German Grand Prix{% include src.html n="2" %}. Dan Gurney gave the marque its first world championship win, in France in 1964, with the BT7{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **The titles.** For the 1966 3-litre formula, he drew relatively simple Repco V8-engined cars, the BT19, BT20 and BT24: Jack Brabham was world champion in 1966, Denny Hulme in 1967{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Production constructor.** Brabhams were also customer cars: 79 F3 and 12 F2 cars left the factory in 1965{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **BT34 (1971).** After Brabham retired, he drew the "lobster claw" BT34, with twin radiators ahead of the front wheels{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Ralt.** After selling the team to Bernie Ecclestone, he founded Ralt{% include src.html n="1,3" %}. The RT1 won the first European F3 championship, in 1975, with Larry Perkins; the [ground-effect]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#effet-de-sol) RT3 followed it{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
- **Honda.** His relationship with Honda began with the 1966 Brabham-Honda F2 cars{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. According to The Race, it was he who put Honda in touch with Frank Williams and [Patrick Head]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/patrick-head.md %}){% include src.html n="1" %}.

## The choice of the spaceframe

- **Eight years without a monocoque.** Tauranac stayed with the [spaceframe chassis]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#chassis-tubulaire) for eight years after the [monocoque]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#monocoque) appeared in F1{% include src.html n="1" %}. In his view, the frame allowed glassfibre panels to be shaped more sympathetically to the aerodynamics than the boxy contours a tub then imposed{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **The BT33 (1970).** His first monocoque, for Jack Brabham's last season, won first time out, in South Africa{% include src.html n="2" %}. According to The Race, it was the new regulations on fuel tanks that forced this construction method on him{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Where sources disagree

- **Taking over the team.** According to the BRDC, Brabham sold him his shareholding at the end of 1969{% include src.html n="2" %}; according to The Race and Goodwood, Tauranac took over the team when Brabham stopped racing, at the end of 1970{% include src.html n="1,3" %}.
- **His departure.** The Race dates the sale to Ecclestone to the end of 1971{% include src.html n="1" %}. Goodwood adds that he stayed on for a time to design the cars, until Ecclestone reorganised the team without him{% include src.html n="3" %}; the BRDC notes that he sold on terms he soon came to regret, and left within a year{% include src.html n="2" %}.
