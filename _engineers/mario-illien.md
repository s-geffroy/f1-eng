## What he did

Mario Illien is the Swiss engineer who founded **Ilmor** with Paul Morgan in 1983. Ilmor designed the Chevrolet V8 that dominated American racing, then the F1 V10s that became the Mercedes engines: with McLaren, they took Mika Häkkinen to the 1998 and 1999 titles.

## Before Ilmor

- Trained at the Biel engineering school, he worked at Mowag, a diesel engine and heavy vehicle manufacturer (Motor Sport, 1991); GrandPrix.com adds that he designed engines for tanks and armoured vehicles there.
- He came to motor racing through Jo Bonnier, for whom he designed a 16-valve cylinder head for the Chrysler Formula 2 engine (Motor Sport, 1991).
- In 1979, he joined Cosworth, where he worked chiefly on the DFY F1 engine and the Sierra 16-valve engine (Motor Sport, 1991).

## Ilmor

- In 1983, Illien and Paul Morgan, both from Cosworth, founded Ilmor in Brixworth, with backing from Roger Penske (Motor Sport, 2001). According to GrandPrix.com, Illien, Morgan, Penske and General Motors each held a quarter.
- According to Illien (formula1.com, 2023), he and Morgan were already doing projects together at Cosworth: he designed parts, Morgan made them. After five years at Cosworth, they chose Indycar, as F1 seemed too hard to enter for a newcomer. In the deal struck with Penske in New Jersey, Morgan and Illien had 25% each and Penske 50%, half of which he gave to the manufacturer he found: Chevrolet, in October 1984.
- The roles were clearly split: Morgan handled manufacturing and the commercial side, Illien the design. Motor Sport writes that Morgan was "turning Illien's designs into reality". At first, Illien says, he designed alone, at home; after three months, he took on two draughtsmen.
- The first product was the 265A, a 2.65-litre 90-degree V8 for Indycar racing. It first ran on the dyno on 16 May 1985 and won its first race in 1987, at Long Beach with Mario Andretti (Motor Sport, 1991; formula1.com, 2023); between 1987 and 1993, Ilmor engines won 86 races, including six Indianapolis 500s (GrandPrix.com).

## F1 V10s and Mercedes

- In 1989, Illien and Morgan decided to build a 3.5-litre V10 for F1. It raced with Leyton House in 1991, then with March and Tyrrell in 1992 (GrandPrix.com). According to Illien, it was [Adrian Newey]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/adrian-newey.md %}), whom he had met at Newman Haas in 1987, who asked him for an engine for Leyton House. That first V10 weighed 126kg according to Illien (about 122kg according to Motor Sport in 1991), against more than 170kg for the competition: less powerful than the Hondas and Renaults, he says, but much lighter. When Leyton House's money ran out in 1991, Illien and Morgan funded the programme themselves; Ivan Capelli scored a point in Hungary.
- In 1993, Ilmor designed Sauber's V10, paid for by Mercedes; at the end of the year, Mercedes-Benz bought out Chevrolet's share and this V10 became the Mercedes V10 (GrandPrix.com). According to Illien, the Chevrolet relationship ended in 1993 over budget, and Mercedes came along in November, for F1 and Indycar. For the 1994 Indianapolis 500, Ilmor designed and built a dedicated pushrod engine in six months, which produced 1024bhp, when Illien had guaranteed Penske "well over 940", and won the race.
- In 1995, Mercedes joined forces with McLaren. The start was difficult; the Mercedes V10's first F1 win came at the end of 1997 (GrandPrix.com).
- **Materials.** In the late 1990s, Ilmor made aluminium-beryllium pistons weighing 167g: a small team developed their heat treatment, forging and diamond-tool machining. Illien also mentions beryllium liners and titanium aluminide valves; in his view, this lead is why such things "got banned eventually" (formula1.com, 2023).
- **Revs and efficiency.** Ilmor went no higher than 19600rpm on the dyno: with a smaller bore than the competition, Illien favoured better combustion, which allowed the cars to race with less fuel. He puts the gain at about 24bhp a year; Ilmor then had up to 504 staff and made almost everything in-house (formula1.com, 2023).
- McLaren-Mercedes then won the 1998 and 1999 drivers' titles with Mika Häkkinen, and the 1998 constructors' title (Motor Sport, 2001).
- Paul Morgan was killed in a flying accident in 2001 (Motor Sport, 2001).
- In April 2005, GrandPrix.com reported a restructuring of Mercedes-Ilmor's technical management: Illien would remain technical director, but stand down at the end of the year.
- According to Illien, Mercedes bought the F1 side of the company in 2005; Ilmor kept its name and carried on in Indycar, with an engine for Honda, and tried a motorcycle project, abandoned for lack of money (formula1.com, 2023).

## Attribution

Ilmor is a company, founded by two people: the engines are Ilmor's, designed under Illien's direction and built under Morgan's. The sources consulted do not detail who in the design office drew each Mercedes V10: Illien himself says he designed alone in Ilmor's early days, but that a modern racing engine involves "several people".

## Where sources disagree

- **Ilmor's founding**: 1983 according to Motor Sport (2001); Illien said in 2023 that the company started "beginning of January 1984".
- **The shareholding**: GrandPrix.com gives a quarter to each of the four partners; Illien describes 25% for himself, 25% for Morgan and 50% for Penske, who gave half of it to the manufacturer. Both versions end in the same split.
- **Early career**: Motor Sport (1991) puts Mowag before motor racing and attributes the Chrysler cylinder head to a commission from Jo Bonnier; according to GrandPrix.com, Illien worked for Bonnier from 1971, then modified the Chrysler-Simca engine for Fred Stalder, then studied in Biel and only joined Mowag in 1977.
- **First run**: 16 May 1985 on the dyno according to Illien; August 1985 according to GrandPrix.com, without saying whether on the dyno or on track.
