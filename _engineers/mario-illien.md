## What he did

Mario Illien is the Swiss engineer who founded **Ilmor** with Paul Morgan in 1983{% include src.html n="2,4" %}. Ilmor designed the Chevrolet [V8]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#architecture-moteur) that dominated American racing, then the F1 V10s that became the Mercedes engines: with McLaren, they took Mika Häkkinen to the 1998 and 1999 titles{% include src.html n="2,3" %}.

## Before Ilmor

- Trained at the Biel engineering school, he worked at Mowag, a diesel engine and heavy vehicle manufacturer (Motor Sport, 1991){% include src.html n="1" %}; GrandPrix.com adds that he designed engines for tanks and armoured vehicles there{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- He came to motor racing through Jo Bonnier, for whom he designed a 16-valve cylinder head for the Chrysler Formula 2 engine (Motor Sport, 1991){% include src.html n="1" %}.
- In 1979, he joined Cosworth, where he worked chiefly on the DFY F1 engine and the Sierra 16-valve engine (Motor Sport, 1991){% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Ilmor

- In 1983, Illien and Paul Morgan, both from Cosworth, founded Ilmor in Brixworth, with backing from Roger Penske (Motor Sport, 2001){% include src.html n="2" %}. According to GrandPrix.com, Illien, Morgan, Penske and General Motors each held a quarter{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- According to Illien (formula1.com, 2023), he and Morgan were already doing projects together at Cosworth: he designed parts, Morgan made them{% include src.html n="5" %}. After five years at Cosworth, they chose IndyCar, as F1 seemed too hard to enter for a newcomer{% include src.html n="5" %}. In the deal struck with Penske in New Jersey, Morgan and Illien had 25% each and Penske 50%, half of which he gave to the manufacturer he found: Chevrolet, in October 1984{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- The roles were clearly split: Morgan handled manufacturing and the commercial side, Illien the design{% include src.html n="1,2,3" %}. Motor Sport writes that Morgan was "turning Illien's designs into reality"{% include src.html n="2" %}. At first, Illien says, he designed alone, at home; after three months, he took on two draughtsmen{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- The first product was the 265A, a 2.65-litre 90-degree V8 for IndyCar racing{% include src.html n="1" %}. It first ran on the dyno on 16 May 1985 and won its first race in 1987, at Long Beach with Mario Andretti (Motor Sport, 1991; formula1.com, 2023){% include src.html n="1,5" %}; between 1987 and 1993, Ilmor engines won 86 races, including six Indianapolis 500s (GrandPrix.com){% include src.html n="2,3" %}.

## F1 V10s and Mercedes

- In 1989, Illien and Morgan decided to build a 3.5-litre V10 for F1{% include src.html n="3" %}. It raced with Leyton House in 1991, then with March and Tyrrell in 1992 (GrandPrix.com){% include src.html n="3" %}. According to Illien, it was [Adrian Newey]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/adrian-newey.md %}), with whom he had worked at Newman Haas in 1987, who asked him for an engine for Leyton House{% include src.html n="5" %}. That first V10 weighed 126 kg according to Illien (about 122 kg according to Motor Sport in 1991), against more than 170 kg for the competition: less powerful than the Hondas and Renaults, he says, but much lighter{% include src.html n="1,5" %}. When Leyton House's money ran out in 1991, Illien and Morgan funded the programme themselves; Ivan Capelli scored a point in Hungary{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- In 1993, Ilmor designed Sauber's V10, paid for by Mercedes; at the end of the year, Mercedes-Benz bought out Chevrolet's share and this V10 became the Mercedes V10 (GrandPrix.com){% include src.html n="3" %}. According to Illien, the Chevrolet relationship ended in 1993 over budget, and Mercedes came along in November, for F1 and IndyCar{% include src.html n="5" %}. For the 1994 Indianapolis 500, Ilmor designed and built a dedicated pushrod engine in six months, which produced 1,024 bhp, when Illien had guaranteed Penske "well over 940", and won the race{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- In 1995, Mercedes joined forces with McLaren{% include src.html n="2,3" %}. The start was difficult; yet the Mercedes V10's first F1 win came at the very first race of 1997, in Australia, with David Coulthard (GrandPrix.com wrongly places it "at the end of 1997"){% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Materials.** In the late 1990s, Ilmor made aluminium-beryllium pistons weighing 167 g: a small team developed their heat treatment, forging and diamond-tool machining{% include src.html n="5" %}. Illien also mentions beryllium liners and titanium aluminide valves; in his view, this lead is why such things "got banned eventually" (formula1.com, 2023){% include src.html n="5" %}.
- **Revs and efficiency.** Ilmor went no higher than 19,600 rpm on the dyno: with a smaller bore than the competition, Illien favoured better combustion, which allowed the cars to race with less fuel{% include src.html n="5" %}. He puts the gain at about 24 bhp a year; Ilmor then had up to 504 staff and made almost everything in-house (formula1.com, 2023){% include src.html n="5" %}.
- McLaren-Mercedes then won the 1998 and 1999 drivers' titles with Mika Häkkinen, and the 1998 constructors' title (Motor Sport, 2001){% include src.html n="2" %}.
- Paul Morgan was killed in a flying accident in 2001 (Motor Sport, 2001){% include src.html n="2" %}.
- In April 2005, GrandPrix.com, citing its own informants, reported a forthcoming restructuring of Mercedes-Ilmor's technical management: Illien would remain technical director, but stand down at the end of the year{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- According to Illien, Mercedes bought the F1 side of the company in 2005; Ilmor kept its name and carried on in IndyCar, with an engine for Honda, and tried a motorcycle project, abandoned for lack of money (formula1.com, 2023){% include src.html n="5" %}.

## Attribution

Ilmor is a company, founded by two people: the engines are Ilmor's, designed under Illien's direction and built under Morgan's{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. The sources consulted do not detail who in the design office drew each Mercedes V10: Illien himself says he designed alone in Ilmor's early days, but that a modern racing engine involves "several people"{% include src.html n="5" %}.

## Where sources disagree

- **Ilmor's founding**: 1983 according to Motor Sport (2001){% include src.html n="2" %}; Illien said in 2023 that the company started "beginning of January 1984"{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- **The shareholding**: GrandPrix.com gives a quarter to each of the four partners{% include src.html n="3" %}; Illien describes 25% for himself, 25% for Morgan and 50% for Penske, who gave half of it to the manufacturer{% include src.html n="5" %}. Both versions end in the same split.
- **Early career**: Motor Sport (1991) mentions Mowag before motor racing, without dating either step, and says he designed the Chrysler cylinder head for Jo Bonnier{% include src.html n="1" %}; according to GrandPrix.com, Illien worked for Bonnier from 1971, then modified the Chrysler-Simca engine for Fred Stalder, then studied in Biel and only joined Mowag in 1977{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **First run**: 16 May 1985 on the dyno according to Illien{% include src.html n="5" %}; August 1985 according to GrandPrix.com, without saying whether on the dyno or on track{% include src.html n="3" %}.
