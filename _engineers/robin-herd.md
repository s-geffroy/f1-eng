## What he did

Robin Herd moved from aerospace to F1: he drew McLaren's first F1 car, then became co-founder and chief designer of March, whose cars raced in F1, F2 and IndyCar{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Technical career

- **Aerospace.** An Oxford graduate in physics and engineering, he joined the Royal Aircraft Establishment at Farnborough and took part in the design of Concorde{% include src.html n="2,3" %}.
- **McLaren (1965-1967).** Recruited in 1965, he drew the team's first F1 car, the M2B, in Mallite, an aircraft-derived material stiffer than aluminium for a given weight{% include src.html n="1" %}. He later said the tub was around eight times stiffer than it needed to be, at the cost of a very difficult construction method{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Cosworth.** After drawing the chassis of the 1968 McLaren M7A, he left for Cosworth to design a four-wheel-drive F1 car, which was tested but never raced{% include src.html n="3" %}. Gordon Coppuck, his former assistant, developed the M7A, which won three Grands Prix in 1968{% include src.html n="2,3" %}.
- **March (1969).** He founded March with Max Mosley, Alan Rees and Graham Coaker; the name is formed from their initials{% include src.html n="2,3" %}.
- **March 701 (1970).** Ten weeks separated the drawing board from the first shakedown{% include src.html n="4" %}. Simple out of necessity, the car took pole at its first race, in South Africa, with Jackie Stewart in a Ken Tyrrell-entered March, then won its second, in Spain{% include src.html n="2,4" %}.
- **March 711 (1971).** Longer wheelbase, low-drag bodywork inspired by Frank Costin, raised front wing: Ronnie Peterson finished second in the championship{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- **After F1.** Herd-designed Marches won several European F2 championships{% include src.html n="2" %}. In IndyCar, March won the Indianapolis 500 every year from 1983 to 1987{% include src.html n="2,3" %}; the 83C was the last he designed, the later cars being the work of [Adrian Newey]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/adrian-newey.md %}), whom Motor Sport says he helped bring on{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
- **A mentor.** Early in their careers, he was a mentor and race engineer to drivers such as Ronnie Peterson, Niki Lauda and Vittorio Brambilla{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## The March 721X: a minimal polar moment

- **The intent.** The 711 had a reduced [polar moment]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#moment-polaire); for 1972, Herd wanted to "go the whole hog" and aim for an ultra-low moment of inertia{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- **The means.** The gearbox, with Alfa Romeo internals in a March-designed casing, copied the layout of the Alfa Romeo Tipo 33/3 sports car{% include src.html n="4" %}; it placed the ratios ahead of the differential, to concentrate mass at the centre of the car{% include src.html n="5" %}. OldRacingCars.com notes that the idea came from the Alfa Romeo T33, and that the Porsche 908/3 already followed the same approach{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- **The failure.** The car understeered{% include src.html n="4,5" %}. Herd blamed the Alfa gearbox, slow to shift, and above all its tiny differential, hence permanently locked{% include src.html n="4" %}; OldRacingCars.com instead blames unsuitable Goodyear tyres{% include src.html n="5" %}. After the Belgian Grand Prix, in June 1972, March dropped it for the 721G, an F1 car built in nine days on an F2 tub{% include src.html n="4,5" %}.
- **What followed.** From then on, March F1 cars were derived from its F2 cars{% include src.html n="4,5" %}. In 1973, James Hunt made his Grand Prix debut in Peterson's former 721G, reworked and run by Hesketh, whose team included [Harvey Postlethwaite]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/harvey-postlethwaite.md %}), coaxed from March{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- **Herd's view.** "I was wrong to do X when I did. It overstretched us."{% include src.html n="4" %}

## Limits of the sources

- **Ground effect in Can-Am.** According to Mark Hughes, Herd designed the McLaren M8 Can-Am car, whose underbody generated [ground effect]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#effet-de-sol) well before F1; Herd recalled measuring the underbody pressure at Goodwood with a vacuum gauge{% include src.html n="1" %}. This site found only one source for this and does not extend it further.
- **The chief designer title.** According to Motor Sport, he joined McLaren with that title, which by his own admission he was in no way equipped to justify{% include src.html n="1" %}. Of all the places he worked, he said, McLaren had "by far" the greatest combined intellect, brought together around Bruce McLaren{% include src.html n="1" %}.
