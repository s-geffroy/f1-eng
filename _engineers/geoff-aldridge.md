## What he did

Geoff Aldridge was a Team Lotus draughtsman: he drew the Lotus 76 chassis, and the Forix database lists him among the designers of the Lotus 77, 79 and 80 (1979){% include src.html n="6,7" %}; OldRacingCars.com places him at Lotus from 1976 to 1979{% include src.html n="3" %}. On the **Lotus 79**, Motor Sport writes that he and [Martin Ogilvie]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/martin-ogilvie.md %}) were charged with turning [Colin Chapman]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/colin-chapman.md %})'s scheme for this 1978 [ground effect]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#effet-de-sol) car into metal{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Career at Lotus

- Born on 27 April 1949 in Norwich, according to OldRacingCars.com, which describes him as mainly involved in [chassis design]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#chassis), within Lotus's engineering group alongside Chapman, [Tony Rudd]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/tony-rudd.md %}) and Ogilvie, on the Lotus 77 and 79{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Lotus 76**: according to Gary Watkins, in Motor Sport, he drew the 76 chassis under [Ralph Bellamy]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ralph-bellamy.md %})'s direction{% include src.html n="6" %}. He remembers Chapman wanting the car 100 lb (45 kg) lighter: "It was his way to give people targets they couldn't hit."{% include src.html n="2,6" %} Like Bellamy, he thinks the car fell victim to internal politics: "It probably wasn't given a fair chance."{% include src.html n="6" %}
- **Lotus 77 (1976)**: at its launch, in October 1975, Motor Sport wrote that the car, conceived by Chapman, was designed in detail by Aldridge and Ogilvie{% include src.html n="5" %}. According to OldRacingCars.com, the team was made up of Aldridge as chassis designer, Ogilvie for suspension and brakes, and Mike Cooke as engineering supervisor{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- **Lotus 79 (1978)**: he drew it with Ogilvie from Chapman's sketch{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Lotus 80 (1979)**: the Forix database, hosted by Autosport, still lists him among the designers, with Ogilvie, under Chapman as technical director, with [Peter Wright]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/peter-wright.md %}) in charge of aerodynamics{% include src.html n="7" %}.

## After Lotus

- **Le Mans 1982**: according to Autosport, he designed the GRID S1, a sports car entered in the 1982 Le Mans 24 Hours{% include src.html n="8" %}.
- **America, then the BTCC**: he went on to design sports cars in America, then worked as a suspension expert in the BTCC for Petronas, according to OldRacingCars.com{% include src.html n="3" %}.

## Where sources disagree

The Forix database gives him the title of "Chief Designer" on the Lotus 77, 79 and 80, but also gives it, for the same cars, to Ogilvie{% include src.html n="7" %}. Motor Sport gives him no title and puts him on an equal footing with Ogilvie{% include src.html n="1,5" %}; Gary Watkins describes him as a Lotus "draughtsman"{% include src.html n="2" %}; OldRacingCars.com speaks of chassis design{% include src.html n="3,4" %}. This site follows Motor Sport's wording: a detail designer, one of two, under Chapman's direction.

His dates at Lotus also differ: OldRacingCars.com places him at Lotus from 1976 to 1979{% include src.html n="3" %}, whereas Motor Sport cites him on the 76, from 1974{% include src.html n="6" %}, and on the 77, unveiled in October 1975{% include src.html n="5" %}. This site goes by the cars, and so places him at Lotus from 1974.
