## What he did

Geoff Aldridge was a Team Lotus chassis designer from 1976 to 1979. On the **Lotus 79**, Motor Sport writes that he and [Martin Ogilvie]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/martin-ogilvie.md %}) were charged with turning [Colin Chapman]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/colin-chapman.md %})'s scheme into metal, the 1978 [ground effect]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#effet-de-sol) car.

## Career at Lotus

- Born on 27 April 1949 in Norwich, according to OldRacingCars.com, which describes him as mainly involved in chassis design, within Lotus's engineering group alongside Chapman, [Tony Rudd]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/tony-rudd.md %}) and Ogilvie.
- **Lotus 76**: Motor Sport quotes him as a Lotus draughtsman. He remembers Chapman wanting the car 100 lb (45 kg) lighter: "It was his way to give people targets they couldn't hit."
- **Lotus 77 (1976)**: according to OldRacingCars.com, the next F1 Lotus was given to a team made up of Aldridge as chassis designer, Ogilvie for suspension and brakes, and Mike Cooke as engineering supervisor. Wikipedia credits the car to Chapman, Aldridge and Ogilvie.
- **Lotus 79 (1978)**: he drew it with Ogilvie from Chapman's sketch.
- **After Lotus**: he went on to design sports cars in America, then worked as a suspension expert in the BTCC for Petronas, according to OldRacingCars.com.

## The sources differ

Wikipedia, as a secondary source, lists him as "Chief Designer" of the Lotus 79. Motor Sport gives him no title and puts him on an equal footing with Ogilvie; OldRacingCars.com speaks of chassis design. This site follows Motor Sport's wording.

## A short entry

Aldridge is documented mainly through mentions in articles devoted to the cars or to other engineers. This site sticks to those facts.
