## What he changed

Bernard Dudot was at the heart of the three changes to the Renault engine that then spread across the whole grid{% include src.html n="2" %}:

**Turbo → [pneumatic valve springs]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#rappel-pneumatique) → V10**

- **The turbo.** According to Renault, every F1 team had a turbo engine during the 1985 season{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Pneumatic valve springs**, introduced on the 1986 EF15 B engine, were, according to Renault, used on every F1 engine from the 1990s onwards{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **The V10.** The 3.5-litre 67° [V10]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#architecture-moteur) was designed by Bernard Dudot (Motor Sport, 1988){% include src.html n="3" %}, heading a team of some twenty engineers{% include src.html n="4" %}; according to Renault, it would impose the V10 architecture on the whole grid{% include src.html n="2" %}.

## Technical career

- Trained at CESTI in Nancy, he joined Alpine in 1967{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- In 1973, [Jean Terramorsi]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/jean-terramorsi.md %}) brought him over from Alpine and sent him to California to study [turbocharging]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#turbocompresseur){% include src.html n="4" %}.
- In April 1975, when Renault decided to enter F1, he took charge of the F1 engine programme, under the technical direction of [François Castaing]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/francois-castaing.md %}){% include src.html n="4,5" %}.
- In 1980, he became technical director of Renault Sport{% include src.html n="4,5" %}.
- In 1986, Renault suspended its involvement{% include src.html n="2,4" %}. At Dudot's insistence, a technical monitoring unit was kept going{% include src.html n="2" %}: it was from this unit that the V10 emerged, designed by Dudot and a team of some twenty engineers at Viry{% include src.html n="2,4" %}.
- Renault's V10 then went on to six consecutive titles in the 1990s{% include src.html n="2" %}.

## Attribution

Renault Sport is an organisation, and the sources show it: GrandPrix.com attributes the idea of turbocharging the racing engine to **Jean Terramorsi**, head of Gordini{% include src.html n="4" %}, and Renault's first F1 turbo engine, the EF1, derived from the 2-litre V6 designed by **François Castaing** and [Jean-Pierre Boudy]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/jean-pierre-boudy.md %}){% include src.html n="1,4" %}. The Renault document presents Dudot as "head of engines"{% include src.html n="2" %}. He is therefore not the lone inventor of these technologies: he is their central technical figure, from their maturation through to the V10.

## Why Tier B, and at its head

Three engine breakthroughs, all spread to the rest of the grid according to Renault's documentation{% include src.html n="2" %}: it is the profile closest to Tier A among the next fifteen. What separates him from it is the collective nature of the achievements.
