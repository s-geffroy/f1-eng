## What he did

Gustav Brunner, an Austrian mechanical engineer from Graz, worked for at least seven F1 teams, from the smallest to Ferrari, before leading design at Toyota for its F1 debut.

## Technical career

- **Beginnings.** A junior designer at McNamara, a builder of Formula Vee and F3 cars, he was hired in 1978 by Günter Schmid's ATS team. Between two spells at ATS, he designed F2 cars for Willi Maurer; Schmid then brought him back to design a BMW-engined ATS for Manfred Winkelhock.
- **RAM.** In 1985 he joined the Hart-engined RAM team.
- **Ferrari, first spell.** He was hired to lead the design team of the **F187**, with which Gerhard Berger won the 1987 Japanese and Australian Grands Prix. The arrival of [John Barnard]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-barnard.md %}) then weakened his position; in 1988 he designed an Indycar that never ran.
- **Rial, Zakspeed.** He designed Schmid's Rial in 1988, then the Yamaha-engined Zakspeed 891 in 1989; GrandPrix.com calls the engine "a disaster".
- **Leyton House.** He worked under [Adrian Newey]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/adrian-newey.md %}) in 1990, became chief engineer after Newey was ousted, then technical director.
- **Minardi and Ferrari.** Having joined Minardi for 1993, he was taken back by Ferrari after half a season and stayed five seasons, according to GrandPrix.com. Craig Scarborough, in Atlas F1, says he was hired in 1993 as chief designer under [Aldo Costa]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/aldo-costa.md %}), with René Hilhorst on aerodynamics. He returned to Minardi as technical director.
- **Minardi, the mechanical solutions.** According to Scarborough, he designed a new car there, with innovations around the gearbox and suspension. A cast titanium gearbox was developed with an Italian casting specialist, CRP Cevolini, which undertook the development and bore its cost. At the rear, he placed the Sachs dampers vertically inside the gear casing, with a scissor-action anti-roll bar. With no more than two days of [wind tunnel]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#soufflerie) time per month, he focused the 2001 PS01 on mechanical gains and simple aerodynamics.
- **Toyota.** In early 2001 he left Minardi for Toyota, in Cologne, a year before the team's 2002 debut; Scarborough writes that he replaced André de Cortanze there. "I spoke to Toyota on Friday, I decided on Saturday, and I moved here on Sunday," he told Atlas F1, adding that his role was increasingly about motivating and managing people rather than designing everything himself.
- **The TF103 (2003).** Toyota presented it as the product of a design group led by Brunner and co-ordinated by Keizo Takahashi. Brunner described it as an evolution of the TF102 rather than a revolution, and credited a "huge aerodynamic step forward" to the team's wind tunnel, fully operational since July (Atlas F1).
- **End at Toyota.** He led the design team until the end of 2005. Toyota then explained that the chief designer post "has become obsolete", with the work split between three parallel groups.
- **The Cologne case (2006).** In January 2006, Autosport reported that Cologne prosecutors had charged him, with Ove Andersson and René Hilhorst, with breaching German competition law: according to the charges, the team knowingly used, in 2002 and 2003, a data analysis program illegally copied by a technician who had come from Ferrari. Brunner denied all the accusations and said no Ferrari data or software had been at Toyota. The article does not give the outcome of the case, and this site has not found it.

## Source discrepancies

- **His title at Toyota.** Toyota's announcement, as carried by Crash.net, makes him chief designer, there to "head up our design team"; Autosport and Reuters call him technical director, succeeding André de Cortanze according to Autosport. Atlas F1, in 2003, and Autosport, in 2006, again call him chief designer.
- **Dates.** GrandPrix.com puts his first arrival at Ferrari in 1985 or 1986. His return to Maranello is dated to the end of 1993 by Crash.net, to half a season after joining Minardi in 1993 by GrandPrix.com, and to the end of 1994 by Atlas F1. His return to Minardi is dated to February 1998 by GrandPrix.com, to 1999 by Atlas F1.
- **List of teams.** Autosport and Crash.net also mention Alfa Romeo, Crash.net Arrows; GrandPrix.com does not.
