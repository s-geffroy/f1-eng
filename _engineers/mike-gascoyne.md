## What he did

Mike Gascoyne, trained in fluid dynamics at Cambridge, moved from aerodynamics to the technical leadership of Jordan, Renault and Toyota{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. What GrandPrix.com mainly credits him with is the group of engineers he put together at Renault{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Technical career

- **McLaren, 1989.** After six years at Cambridge and a spell at Westland, he joined [Steve Nichols]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/steve-nichols.md %})'s design team, working in the wind tunnel at the National Physical Laboratory in Teddington{% include src.html n="1" %}. According to Toyota, it was [Bob Bell]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/bob-bell.md %}) who hired him there{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Tyrrell.** In late 1989 he joined [Harvey Postlethwaite]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/harvey-postlethwaite.md %}) at Tyrrell and worked on the **Tyrrell 019**, "revolutionary" according to GrandPrix.com: chassis dynamics, computer simulations and aerodynamic mapping{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Sauber.** He followed Postlethwaite to Sauber in June 1991 and stayed on, after Postlethwaite left for Ferrari, as aerodynamics engineer under Nichols{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Back at Tyrrell.** In October 1993 Postlethwaite called him back{% include src.html n="1" %}. He stayed until British American Racing bought the team in 1998{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Jordan.** Arriving in June 1998 as chief designer under Gary Anderson, he was left in charge of the technical department when Anderson left at the end of the year{% include src.html n="1" %}. The 1999 **Jordan 199** came from a team he led, with Tim Holloway as engineering director, Mark Smith as chief designer and John Iley on aerodynamics; Heinz-Harald Frentzen won two Grands Prix and Jordan finished third in the constructors' championship{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Benetton-Renault.** Appointed technical director shortly before the 2001 season, he built a new engineering group there; according to GrandPrix.com, this group built the successful Renault chassis of 2003 to 2006{% include src.html n="1" %}. Bob Bell was his deputy technical director{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Toyota.** He left Renault in summer 2003 and became Toyota's technical director, chassis, on 1 December 2003{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. There he described himself as responsible for "all areas of the chassis design", from aerodynamics to simulation, as well as race engineering and strategy{% include src.html n="2" %}. Arriving too late for the 2004 car, he was responsible for the **TF105**, which took Toyota to fourth in the constructors' championship{% include src.html n="1" %}. He was dropped early in 2006{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Spyker.** In September 2006 he was announced as Spyker's chief technology officer{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Attribution

GrandPrix.com credits the Renault chassis of 2003 to 2006 to the group he built, not to him alone: he left the team in summer 2003, and Bob Bell, his deputy, became its technical director{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Likewise, the Jordan 199 was the work of a team he led{% include src.html n="3" %}.

## Where sources disagree

His role on returning to Tyrrell varies: chief designer according to GrandPrix.com, deputy technical director under Postlethwaite according to Toyota{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
