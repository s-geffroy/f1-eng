## What he did

Mike Gascoyne, trained in fluid dynamics at Cambridge, moved from aerodynamics to the technical leadership of Jordan, Renault and Toyota. What GrandPrix.com mainly credits him with is the group of engineers he put together at Renault.

## Technical career

- **McLaren, 1989.** After six years at Cambridge and a spell at Westland, he joined [Steve Nichols]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/steve-nichols.md %})'s design team, working in the wind tunnel at the National Physical Laboratory in Teddington. According to Toyota, it was [Bob Bell]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/bob-bell.md %}) who hired him there.
- **Tyrrell.** In late 1989 he joined [Harvey Postlethwaite]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/harvey-postlethwaite.md %}) at Tyrrell and worked on the **Tyrrell 019**, "revolutionary" according to GrandPrix.com: chassis dynamics, computer simulations and aerodynamic mapping.
- **Sauber.** He followed Postlethwaite to Sauber in June 1991 and stayed on, after Postlethwaite left for Ferrari, as aerodynamics engineer under Nichols.
- **Back at Tyrrell.** In October 1993 Postlethwaite called him back. He stayed until British American Racing bought the team in 1998.
- **Jordan.** Arriving in June 1998 as chief designer under Gary Anderson, he was left in charge of the technical office when Anderson left at the end of the year. The 1999 **Jordan 199** came from a team he led, with Tim Holloway as engineering director, Mark Smith as chief designer and John Iley on aerodynamics; Heinz-Harald Frentzen won two Grands Prix and Jordan finished third in the constructors' championship.
- **Benetton-Renault.** Technical director shortly before the 2001 season, he built a new engineering group there; according to GrandPrix.com, this group built the successful Renault chassis of 2003 to 2006. Bob Bell was his deputy technical director.
- **Toyota.** He left Renault in summer 2003 and became Toyota's technical director, chassis, on 1 December 2003. There he described himself as responsible for "all areas of the chassis design", from aerodynamics to simulation, as well as race engineering and strategy. Arriving too late for the 2004 car, he signed the **TF105**, which took Toyota to fourth in the constructors' championship. He was dropped early in 2006.
- **Spyker.** In September 2006 he was announced as Spyker's chief technology officer.

## Attribution

GrandPrix.com credits the Renault chassis of 2003 to 2006 to the group he built, not to him alone: he left the team in summer 2003, and Bob Bell, his deputy, became its technical director. Likewise, the Jordan 199 was the work of a team he led.

## Source discrepancies

His role on returning to Tyrrell varies: chief designer according to GrandPrix.com, deputy technical director under Postlethwaite according to Toyota.
