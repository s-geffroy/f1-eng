## What he did

Jean Terramorsi is an **executive**, not a designer: as head of Gordini, he was the man who set Renault on the turbo path.

- GrandPrix.com credits him with the idea of turbocharging modern racing engines. Motor Sport's account is rather that it was [Bernard Dudot]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/bernard-dudot.md %})'s tests that convinced him: this makes him the decision-maker rather than the inventor.
- In 1973, he recruited Dudot at Alpine and sent him to California to study [turbocharging]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#turbocompresseur).
- Motor Sport attributes Alpine-Renault's success to the foresight of this colourful team chief, and to the vigour of the young Dudot.
- In late 1975, he had to step down for health reasons.
- In 1976, at Renault's Product Division, in charge of small-series production, he launched the Renault 5 Turbo project. He died on 26 August 1976.

## Attribution

The idea of the turbo, the decision to pursue it and its execution belong to different people: Terramorsi decided, Dudot studied turbocharging, and the base V6 was designed by [François Castaing]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/francois-castaing.md %}) and Jean-Pierre Boudy.
