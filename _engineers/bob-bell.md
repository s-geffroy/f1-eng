## What he did

Bob Bell, a Northern Irish engineer, was the aerodynamicist of the 1988 McLaren MP4/4, then technical director of Renault and of Mercedes{% include src.html n="1,5" %}. Mark Hughes sums up his record in two cars, the MP4/4 and the 2014 Mercedes W05: "Two cars, a total of 35 races – and they won 31 of them."{% include src.html n="1" %}

## Technical career

- **McLaren.** With a doctorate from Queen's University Belfast, he was recruited by [John Barnard]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-barnard.md %}) and entered F1 in 1982{% include src.html n="1,3" %}. He stayed fourteen years according to Joe Saward, set up and ran the research and development department, and worked on materials, structures and the company's first [CAD]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#cao) system{% include src.html n="1,3" %}.
- **The MP4/4 (1988).** He was "the aerodynamicist in charge": "I did the aero design, drew the wind tunnel model bits, oversaw the [wind tunnel]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#soufflerie) tests."{% include src.html n="1" %} The Honda engine's lower crankshaft let him sweep up the underfloor for more [ground effect]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#effet-de-sol){% include src.html n="1" %}. According to Mark Hughes, he was then the only aerodynamicist in the whole company{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Benetton, Jordan.** He left McLaren for a land speed record project that never materialised, moved to Benetton in 1997, then joined [Mike Gascoyne]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/mike-gascoyne.md %}) at Jordan in 1999{% include src.html n="3,4,5" %}.
- **Renault.** Back at Enstone in 2001 as Gascoyne's deputy technical director, he succeeded him as technical director{% include src.html n="3,5" %}. After Flavio Briatore and [Pat Symonds]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/pat-symonds.md %}) left in the wake of the Singapore affair, he ran the team on an interim basis until Éric Boullier arrived at the start of 2010, then stepped down as managing director in October 2010{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- **Mercedes.** Technical director from April 2011, in charge of the chassis group, he freed [Ross Brawn]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ross-brawn.md %}), who admitted covering the role "not effectively enough"{% include src.html n="5,6" %}. According to Mark Hughes, he then recruited [Geoff Willis]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/geoff-willis.md %}) and [Aldo Costa]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/aldo-costa.md %}), who reported to him{% include src.html n="1,7" %}; on the W05 he describes himself as the overseer of both strands, the chassis at Brackley and [Andy Cowell]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/andy-cowell.md %})'s engine at Brixworth, making sure they gelled{% include src.html n="1" %}. Having resigned in December 2013, he handed his responsibilities to [Paddy Lowe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/paddy-lowe.md %}), as Mercedes announced in April 2014, and the post was not filled; his departure from the team was set for the end of November 2014{% include src.html n="8,9" %}.
- **Back at Renault.** He returned in February 2016, after the Lotus buy-out, as chief technical officer, and planned Renault's return as a works team{% include src.html n="10" %}. In July 2018 he moved to a part-time technical advisor role{% include src.html n="10" %}.

## Attribution

On the MP4/4, Bell claims the aerodynamic design, not the concept: "The ownership of the car conceptually is a pretty difficult thing to pin down."{% include src.html n="1,2" %} He credits [Gordon Murray]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gordon-murray.md %}) with pushing to lay the driver down further, without knowing how much of the low position was natural evolution{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Autosport lists him among those who designed the car or did its detail work, with [Steve Nichols]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/steve-nichols.md %}), [Matthew Jeffreys]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/matthew-jeffreys.md %}) and [David North]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/david-north.md %}){% include src.html n="2" %}.

On the W05, his role was that of a technical director: he organised and oversaw; he was not the author of the power unit{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Where sources disagree

- **Renault technical director**: from 2002 according to Autosport{% include src.html n="4" %}; from 2003, replacing Gascoyne, according to Auto123{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- **Arrival at Mercedes**: April 2011 according to Auto123{% include src.html n="5" %}; Motor Sport writes that Brawn recruited him in 2012{% include src.html n="1" %}, which sits badly with Costa and Willis being announced as his reports in September 2011{% include src.html n="7" %}.
- **Leaving Mercedes**: April 2014 according to F1i, which then mentions a brief spell at Manor Marussia{% include src.html n="10" %}. Mercedes' April 2014 announcement (formula1.com, Auto123) separates the two dates: his responsibilities passed to Lowe from April, while his departure was set for the end of November 2014{% include src.html n="8,9" %}.
