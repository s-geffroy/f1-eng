## What he did

Tony Southgate is a prolific F1 designer of the 1970s, from BRM to Arrows.

## Technical career

- **BRM.** Chief designer for the 1970 season; at Spa, Pedro Rodriguez gave BRM its first win in four years. With his 1971 P160, the team finished second in the constructors' championship; in 1972, Jean-Pierre Beltoise won again with a revised P160.
- **Shadow.** He built the DN1 prototype in his garage, then the DN3 (1974) and DN5 (1975).
- **Lotus (1976-1977).** He worked on the Lotus 77 and, with [Peter Wright]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/peter-wright.md %}), on the development of the 78; from 1976, he was race engineer on Gunnar Nilsson's car.
- **Arrows.** With his first Arrows judged to have been copied from the Shadow DN9, he drew a new car in 57 days. The 1979 Arrows A2 extended an idea discussed at Lotus: a car shaped entirely like a wing, without [wings]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#aileron). According to Southgate, it produced 1,600 lb of [downforce]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#appui) at 150 mph, but its excessively high centre of gravity heavily penalised it.
- **Theodore.** He designed the TY01 for the 1981 season.

## The Lotus 78: setting the roles straight

Formula 1 credits the design of the Lotus 78 to [Ralph Bellamy]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ralph-bellamy.md %}) and Tony Southgate. GrandPrix.com instead places him on the development of the 78, alongside Peter Wright; according to Motor Sport, Bellamy drew the car and [Martin Ogilvie]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/martin-ogilvie.md %}) the suspension. This site adopts that division of roles.
