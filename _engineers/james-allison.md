## What he did

James Allison is an aerodynamicist trained at Benetton who became technical director of Renault and then Lotus, of Ferrari, and finally of Mercedes{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Technical career

- **Benetton.** A Cambridge graduate, he joined Benetton in 1991 as a junior aerodynamic designer{% include src.html n="1" %}. He moved to Larrousse in 1993 as head of aerodynamics, then returned to Benetton, where he contributed to the 1994 and 1995 titles{% include src.html n="1" %}. He became its head of aerodynamics in 1997{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Ferrari, first spell.** He joined Ferrari in 1999 as trackside aerodynamicist and stayed six years, through the run of constructors' titles from 1999 to 2004{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Renault, then Lotus.** Back at Enstone in 2005 as Renault's deputy technical director, he contributed to the 2005 and 2006 titles with Fernando Alonso{% include src.html n="1" %}. Technical director from 2009, he saw the team through its transition to Lotus, which won again in Abu Dhabi in 2012 with Kimi Räikkönen{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Ferrari, second spell.** He returned to Maranello in 2013 as technical director; Sebastian Vettel won three races there in 2015{% include src.html n="1" %}. He left Ferrari in 2016{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
- **Mercedes.** Mercedes created the post of technical director for him, from March 2017, after [Paddy Lowe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/paddy-lowe.md %}) left in January{% include src.html n="2,3" %}; he reported directly to Toto Wolff{% include src.html n="2" %}. The team won five constructors' and four drivers' titles in that period{% include src.html n="1" %}. He became chief technical officer, a new post, in mid-2021, then returned to the role of technical director in April 2023{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## DAS (2020)

- In 2020 winter testing, Mercedes revealed DAS, a steering wheel that is pulled and pushed{% include src.html n="4" %}. Allison described it as "an extra dimension for the steering, for the driver" and said the rules were "pretty clear" about what is permitted{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- According to The Race, Mercedes wanted to introduce it as early as 2019: the FIA accepted the principle but rejected lever actuation and required the whole wheel to move{% include src.html n="5" %}. It was chief designer John Owen and his mechanical design team who drew the final version; Allison called Owen "very inventive"{% include src.html n="5" %}. The system raced in 2020 before being banned for 2021{% include src.html n="5" %}.

DAS was therefore a team effort: Allison presented it and argued its principle with the FIA; the mechanical solution was the work of John Owen's group{% include src.html n="4,5" %}.
