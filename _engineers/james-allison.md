## What he did

James Allison is an aerodynamicist trained at Benetton who became technical director of Renault and then Lotus, of Ferrari, and finally of Mercedes.

## Technical career

- **Benetton.** A Cambridge graduate, he joined Benetton in 1991 as a junior aerodynamic designer. He moved to Larrousse in 1993 as head of aerodynamics, then returned to Benetton, where he contributed to the 1994 and 1995 titles. He became its head of aerodynamics in 1997.
- **Ferrari, first spell.** He joined Ferrari in 1999 as trackside aerodynamicist and stayed six years, through the run of constructors' titles from 1999 to 2004.
- **Renault, then Lotus.** Back at Enstone in 2005 as Renault's deputy technical director, he contributed to the 2005 and 2006 titles with Fernando Alonso. Technical director from 2009, he saw the team through its transition to Lotus, which won again in Abu Dhabi in 2012 with Kimi Räikkönen.
- **Ferrari, second spell.** He returned to Maranello in 2013 as technical director; Sebastian Vettel won three races there in 2015. He left Ferrari in 2016.
- **Mercedes.** Mercedes created the post of technical director for him, from March 2017, after [Paddy Lowe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/paddy-lowe.md %}) left in January; he reported directly to Toto Wolff. The team won five constructors' and four drivers' titles in that period. He became chief technical officer, a new post, in mid-2021, then took back the technical director role in April 2023.

## DAS (2020)

- In 2020 winter testing, Mercedes revealed DAS, a steering wheel that is pulled and pushed. Allison described it as "an extra dimension for the steering, for the driver" and said the rules were "pretty clear" about what is permitted.
- According to The Race, Mercedes wanted to introduce it as early as 2019: the FIA accepted the principle but rejected lever actuation and required the whole wheel to move. It was chief designer John Owen and his mechanical design team who drew the final version; Allison calls him "very inventive". The system raced in 2020 before being banned for 2021.

DAS was therefore a team effort: Allison presented it and argued its principle with the FIA; the mechanical solution belongs to John Owen's group.
