## What he did

Matthew Jeffreys was a McLaren design engineer from 1984 to 2005{% include src.html n="1" %}. On the 1988 McLaren MP4/4, he was project leader for the [monocoque]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#monocoque) and front suspension{% include src.html n="1" %}. Since at least 2021, he has publicly challenged [Gordon Murray]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gordon-murray.md %})'s account of how the car came about{% include src.html n="1,5" %}.

## At McLaren

- **1984.** He joined McLaren International after his degree: "right from the age of 12, I had always wanted to work in F1," he told Crash.net in 2001{% include src.html n="3" %}. A photo published by Motor Sport shows him in [John Barnard]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-barnard.md %})'s design team in 1984{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- **The MP4/4 (1988).** Autosport describes him as a close friend of [Steve Nichols]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/steve-nichols.md %}) and as "responsible for the design of the MP4/4 from the front bulkhead forward"{% include src.html n="2" %}. He describes himself as project leader for the monocoque and front suspension design{% include src.html n="1" %}. Motor Sport calls him a "senior design engineer"{% include src.html n="5,6" %}; according to Nichols (Motor Sport, 2021), Jeffreys focused on the front end of the car and its integrated front suspension, while Nichols handled the monocoque, overall layout and integration{% include src.html n="5" %}.
- **The drivers' position.** With his colleague Hugh Moran, he set the seat-back angle in the monocoque, and therefore the drivers' seating position{% include src.html n="1" %}. According to him, Alain Prost and Ayrton Senna sat in a monocoque mock-up in January and February 1988; seats were then made for MP4/4-02, ahead of the first test at Imola, on 23 March{% include src.html n="1" %}. Prost's head sat about 3in further forward than Senna's, but their eyes were at the same height{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **2001.** Crash.net describes him as project leader for [chassis design]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#chassis){% include src.html n="3" %}. His group was responsible for the monocoque, the driver installation and controls, and the front and rear wings{% include src.html n="3" %}. "The whole design process starts with the aerodynamic shape of the car," he explains{% include src.html n="3" %}.

## On Barnard and Murray

Jeffreys compares the methods of the two technical directors he worked under{% include src.html n="2" %}. With Barnard, "it was important to him that he had his stamp on everything."{% include src.html n="2" %} Murray "left us to our own devices much more."{% include src.html n="2" %} If there was one area Murray got involved in technically on the MP4/4, he says, "it was in the gearbox."{% include src.html n="2" %}

## What he disputes

**In 2021.** After an interview in which Murray claimed the design of the MP4/4, Jeffreys replied in a letter published by Motor Sport in October 2021: Murray and the design team must have been "living in parallel universes", as Murray's version of events was not the same as his own, or that of the former colleagues he had spoken to{% include src.html n="5" %}. The James Elson article that followed, the same year, adds more:

- all 17 members of the design team had already written to Martin Whitmarsh, then McLaren's new director, listing 82 inaccuracies in Ian Bamsey's book crediting the car to Murray{% include src.html n="5" %};
- Jeffreys recounts that Murray, sitting at the back of the room during a talk he gave with Nichols at Brooklands, handed them two formal legal notices at the end, objecting to what they had said in Steve Rendle's Haynes manual, for which Rendle had interviewed all 17{% include src.html n="5" %};
- Jeffreys rejects any link with the BT55: "None of us were looking at BT55 drawings and we wouldn't have wanted to be either – it was a disaster."{% include src.html n="5" %}

**In 2026.** In a Motor Sport article on the Brabham BT55, Murray calls the MP4/4 "exactly the same layout"{% include src.html n="4" %}. Jeffreys replied in a letter published in November 2026{% include src.html n="1" %}:

- he points out that at least 16 other former McLaren staff who worked on the MP4/4 have, like him, disputed Murray's account for years{% include src.html n="1" %};
- neither he nor Nichols recalls Murray showing them BT55 drawings{% include src.html n="1" %};
- Nichols, chief designer appointed by Murray, was in his words "100% responsible for its architecture, layout and overall design", a responsibility conferred by Ron Dennis and Murray in August 1987 in a staff memo that he says he still has{% include src.html n="1" %};
- he also disputes Murray's story that Prost refused to drive lying down: "I think Gordon's memory may be failing him."{% include src.html n="1" %}

## Attribution

Jeffreys is both a witness and a party to the dispute: he defends the version of Nichols, his friend{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Motor Sport, in 2024, speaks of an "internal faction" led by Nichols and Jeffreys{% include src.html n="6" %}. Autosport, which compared the accounts in 2017, lists him among those who designed the MP4/4 or did its detail work, with Nichols, [Bob Bell]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/bob-bell.md %}) and [David North]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/david-north.md %}), while crediting the concept mainly to Murray{% include src.html n="2" %}. This site presents both versions.
