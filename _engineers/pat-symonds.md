## What he did

Pat Symonds spent the first part of his career with the Enstone team, from Toleman to Renault: race engineer, head of research and development, then technical director{% include src.html n="1,3" %}. He finished as chief technical officer of Formula 1 itself, involved in writing the 2022 and 2026 regulations{% include src.html n="4" %}.

## Technical career

- **Training.** A Ford apprentice, he earned an MSc in automotive engineering at Cranfield in 1976, then designed Formula Ford cars for Hawke and Royale, where he met [Rory Byrne]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/rory-byrne.md %}){% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Toleman.** In 1979 Byrne took him to Toleman for a Formula 2 car{% include src.html n="1" %}. A full-time member of the F1 team from 1982, he was a race engineer, notably for Ayrton Senna's debut season in 1984{% include src.html n="1,3" %}.
- **Benetton.** After Benetton took over Toleman, he stayed on as a race engineer{% include src.html n="1,3" %}. In 1991 he left with Byrne for an F1 project at Reynard, which failed, then returned{% include src.html n="1,3" %}. He then became Michael Schumacher's race engineer and head of research and development, through the title-winning seasons of 1994 and 1995{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Technical director.** He succeeded [Ross Brawn]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ross-brawn.md %}) in the post in November 1996{% include src.html n="1,3" %}. When [Mike Gascoyne]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/mike-gascoyne.md %}) arrived in 2001, he became executive director of engineering, a role he kept under [Bob Bell]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/bob-bell.md %}){% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Singapore 2008.** After it emerged in 2009 that Nelson Piquet Jr had crashed deliberately at the 2008 Singapore Grand Prix, Symonds and Flavio Briatore were banned from F1; he left Renault{% include src.html n="3,4" %}.
- **Marussia, Williams.** A technical consultant to Marussia from 2011, he was its de facto technical director in 2013, before joining Williams as chief technical officer on 19 August 2013{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Formula 1.** F1's chief technical officer from 2017, he contributed to the 2022 rules, the era of the return of [ground effect]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#effet-de-sol), and to the design of the 2026 cars{% include src.html n="4" %}. He left the post in May 2024, seven years on, once the 2026 chassis rules were all but finalised{% include src.html n="4" %}.

## Witness to Imola 1994

In 1994 Symonds was head of research and development at Benetton{% include src.html n="2" %}. Interviewed by formula1.com in 2019, he described the response that followed Imola{% include src.html n="2" %}:

- the first job was not to make the cars safer but to **slow them down**: a shorter diffuser, altered front wing endplates, then the plank under the floor{% include src.html n="2" %};
- next came wheel tethers, "to prevent wheels flying loose in an impact", and impact tests at ever higher speeds{% include src.html n="2" %};
- these measures, he said, protected not only the drivers but the spectators too; he placed the halo in this legacy{% include src.html n="2" %}.

This is the testimony of someone who was there at the time, not a personal contribution to the 1994 rules, which the FIA wrote.

## Attribution

None of the sources consulted credits him personally with a car or an innovation. His role on the title-winning Benettons was that of a race engineer and head of research, alongside Byrne and Brawn{% include src.html n="1" %}; on the 2022 and 2026 rules, that of a contributor within the F1 organisation{% include src.html n="4" %}.
