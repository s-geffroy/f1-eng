## What he did

Maurice Philippe was Team Lotus's chief designer at the end of the 1960s. He is generally credited, with [Colin Chapman]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/colin-chapman.md %}), with the 1970 **Lotus 72**; how much each contributed is disputed.

## Technical career

- **De Havilland.** He was a draughtsman at de Havilland in the early 1950s; Motor Sport sees there the origin of his knowledge.
- **Lotus.** He took part in the design of the Lotus 49 and 72, and of many other Chapman projects: the 43, the 56 Indianapolis turbine car, the four-wheel-drive 63, the 64.
- **Vel's Parnelli Jones (1971-1972).** In the autumn of 1971, he left Lotus for this American USAC team; Martin Waide replaced him at Team Lotus. His first car brought Joe Leonard the 1972 USAC title. Its side-mounted dihedral wings caused a stir but never raced: they were removed in the first days of testing.
- **Tyrrell (from 1977).** In mid-1977, [Derek Gardner]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/derek-gardner.md %}) hired him to install the Renault turbo V6 in the six-wheeled Tyrrell; the project came to nothing. After Gardner left, he widened the P34's front track and moved its oil radiators forward, without success. Ken Tyrrell then entrusted him with the P34's replacement, the start of a ten-year relationship.
- **Late career.** When he died, on 5 June 1989, he was working on the March-Alfa Romeo IndyCar project.

## The Lotus 72

- **The choices.** The 72 stood out for its wedge shape: the radiators moved from the nose to the sides, behind the driver, shifting weight rearwards for traction. The brakes were mounted inboard at the front as well as the rear, the torsion-bar suspension was rising-rate, and the geometry countered dive under braking and squat under acceleration.
- **The record.** According to Autosport, it won two drivers' titles, three constructors' titles and 20 Grands Prix over six seasons.
- **An inherited shape.** According to Martin Waide, the wedge was already ingrained in Lotus design culture: he credits its origins to Richard Parker, an engineer at Lotus Components.
- **The successor.** [Ralph Bellamy]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ralph-bellamy.md %}), who arrived in late 1972, drew the 76, which he describes as "a tidied-up 72".

## Where sources disagree

- **Who designed the 72?** Mike Pilbeam, a member of the design team with Philippe and Geoff Ferris, recalls that Chapman shut himself away for two weeks and handed them quarter-scale drawings to "make work": in his view Philippe "undoubtedly" had an input, but most of the ideas were Chapman's. In 1972, Motor Sport noted that Lotus now presented Philippe as merely the draughtsman of the 72; Philippe maintained that it was, like the cars before it, a joint project with Chapman. This site keeps the co-credit, without settling each man's share.
- **Spelling.** Sources write both Philippe and Phillippe.
