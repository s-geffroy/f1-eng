## What he did

Alan Jenkins was [John Barnard]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/john-barnard.md %})'s deputy in the early days of the carbon [monocoque]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#monocoque) McLaren, then technical director of several teams{% include src.html n="1,2,3" %}.

## Technical career

- **McLaren.** Recruited in 1979 by Project 4, he became Barnard's right-hand man when Project 4 took over McLaren, and remained his deputy until 1984{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. He was there in the early days of the MP4/1, when [Steve Nichols]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/steve-nichols.md %}) joined the team{% include src.html n="3" %}. As John Watson's race engineer, he played a part in Watson's win at Long Beach in 1983, from 22nd on the grid{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Penske.** He designed the PC15 and PC16 IndyCars, sophisticated but seldom winners{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
- **Onyx.** He designed the ORE-1, the team's first F1 car, in 1989{% include src.html n="1,4" %}.
- **Footwork.** As technical director, he designed the Porsche-engined FA12{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Stewart.** As technical director, he designed the 1997 SF1, then the SF3 before leaving{% include src.html n="1,5" %}. He worked there with 50% scale models, a practice still recent in F1 (only Williams and Ferrari used it at the time){% include src.html n="6" %}; according to Jenkins, the use of these models and of computational fluid dynamics, a technique that was still new, was a deliberate choice by the team{% include src.html n="6" %}.
