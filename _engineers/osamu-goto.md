## What he did

Osamu Goto is the engineer who led Honda's F1 engine programme through the title years with Williams and then McLaren: a **team leader**, rather than the identified designer of a particular engine.

## At Honda

- Born on 27 November 1948 in Tokyo, he graduated in aeronautical engineering, according to OldRacingCars.com.
- After graduating in March 1970, he joined Honda's research department the following month; he first specialised in exhaust emissions (GrandPrix.com).
- In 1984, promoted to chief engineer, he joined Honda's racing team to develop the 1.5-litre [turbo]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#turbocompresseur) engine; a year later, he became racing team leader (GrandPrix.com).
- In 1986 and 1987, the engineering team he led won the constructors' title with Williams (GrandPrix.com).
- That success put him in charge of all Honda F1 engine development, notably the normally-aspirated [V10]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#architecture-moteur) (GrandPrix.com). According to him, Honda had already been working on that V10 for two years by 1988 (Autosport).
- For 1988, Honda stayed with the turbo. Gordon Murray says he got Honda to lower the engine substantially for the McLaren MP4/4; Goto confirms that Murray wanted to keep the driving position of the Brabham BT55, "therefore he'd like a lower engine" (Autosport).
- In 1989, Motor Sport described him as leading the McLaren-Honda engine project.

## After Honda

- **McLaren.** According to GrandPrix.com, Ron Dennis recruited him to McLaren as Executive Engineer at the end of 1991, although what he actually did there was never fully explained. OldRacingCars.com judges the spell unsuccessful.
- **Ferrari.** He joined Ferrari in January 1994 to work on the new V10, and went on to head its engine research and development (GrandPrix.com, June 1996).
- **Ford, a rumour.** In June 1996, GrandPrix.com tipped him to leave, to help Cosworth develop Ford's new V10. The October 1996 report sent him elsewhere; this site does not treat that move as fact.
- **Petronas and Sauber.** In October 1996, GrandPrix.com reported that the Malaysian government appeared to have decided to set up its own engine company, and that Goto seemed to be the man chosen: he was to establish a facility in England to design and build a Petronas V10, which Sauber hoped to use in 1998, and up to thirty young Malaysian engineers were expected to join him. At the start of 1997, he became head of Sauber's engine department (GrandPrix.com).
- **Afterwards.** According to OldRacingCars.com, he now runs his own engineering consultancy.

## Attribution

Honda's engines of this period were the work of a whole department; the sources consulted make Goto its leader, without crediting him with the design of a specific engine. On the MP4/4, the request for a lower engine came from [Gordon Murray]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gordon-murray.md %}), while authorship of the car itself remains disputed between Murray and [Steve Nichols]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/steve-nichols.md %}). Finally, GrandPrix.com's profile lists constructors' titles from 1986 to 1991, yet speaks of "five" consecutive titles for Honda: the count is inconsistent within the source itself.

## Where sources disagree

- **Time at the head of the F1 programme**: six years according to GrandPrix.com in June 1996; OldRacingCars.com speaks of about five years of success.
- **Between McLaren and Ferrari**: GrandPrix.com's profile and OldRacingCars.com place a brief return to Japan, at Tokyo R&D (one month according to GrandPrix.com); GrandPrix.com's 1996 news items do not mention it.

## Limits of the sources

Goto is documented mainly through biographical profiles, short news items and mentions in articles devoted to McLaren. This site sticks to those facts.
