## What he did

Osamu Goto is the engineer who led Honda's F1 engine programme through the title years with Williams and then McLaren: a **team leader**, rather than the identified designer of a particular engine.

## At Honda

- After graduating in March 1970, he joined Honda's research department the following month; he first specialised in exhaust emissions (GrandPrix.com).
- In 1984, promoted to chief engineer, he joined Honda's racing team to develop the 1.5-litre [turbo]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#turbocompresseur) engine; a year later, he became racing team leader (GrandPrix.com).
- In 1986 and 1987, the engineering team he led won the constructors' title with Williams (GrandPrix.com).
- That success put him in charge of all Honda F1 engine development, notably the normally-aspirated V10 (GrandPrix.com). According to him, Honda had already been working on that V10 for a couple of years by 1988 (Autosport).
- For 1988, Honda stayed with the turbo. Gordon Murray says he got Honda to lower the engine substantially for the McLaren MP4/4; Goto confirms that Murray wanted to keep the driving position of the Brabham BT55, "therefore he'd like a lower engine" (Autosport).
- In 1989, Motor Sport described him as leading the McLaren-Honda engine project.

## After Honda

According to GrandPrix.com, Ron Dennis recruited him to McLaren as Executive Engineer at the end of 1991, although what he actually did there was never fully explained. He then moved to Ferrari as head of engine research and development, and, at the start of 1997, became head of Sauber's engine department.

## Attribution

Honda's engines of this period were the work of a whole department; the sources consulted make Goto its leader, without crediting him with the design of a specific engine. On the MP4/4, the request for a lower engine came from [Gordon Murray]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gordon-murray.md %}), while authorship of the car itself remains disputed between him and [Steve Nichols]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/steve-nichols.md %}). Finally, GrandPrix.com's profile lists constructors' titles from 1986 to 1991, yet speaks of "five" consecutive titles for Honda: the count is inconsistent within the source itself.

## A short entry

Goto is documented mainly through one biographical profile and mentions in articles devoted to McLaren. This site sticks to those facts.
