## What he did

Gianni Lancia was not a design-office engineer: he was the **head of Lancia**, son of the founder Vincenzo Lancia, running the family company with his mother Adele. He decided to take Lancia into F1 and commissioned the **Lancia D50**, designed by a team led by [Vittorio Jano]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/vittorio-jano.md %}).

## The commissioner

- After 1952, he decided the works team would drop the Aurelia coupés for a true series of sports racing cars; according to Doug Nye, Lancia then won the Carrera Panamericana, the Targa Florio and the Mille Miglia in 1953-1954.
- The next step of his ambitions was the new 2.5-litre F1: the experimental department started the programme in August 1953. He set a debut at the French Grand Prix of 4 July 1954 as the target; the D50 only raced late in the season, at the Spanish Grand Prix in Barcelona.
- According to Chris Nixon, he persuaded Alberto Ascari to leave Scuderia Ferrari by outlining his plans and revealing the details of the new car; Ascari brought Gigi Villoresi with him.

## His part in the design

The two Motor Sport articles do not say the same thing, and this site does not decide between them.

- **Doug Nye** credits him with no design work: the car's principles are Jano's, and the engine was detailed with [Ettore Zaccone Mina]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ettore-zaccone-mina.md %}), and he credits Jano with the idea of the pannier tanks.
- **Chris Nixon**, reporting Zaccone Mina's account, gives him a more direct role. The [stressed engine]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#moteur-porteur) and the placing of the fuel tanks came out of the team's discussions, "with Gianni Lancia making the final decisions". Several innovations are said to have come from his "fertile brain", and the elegant **pannier fuel tanks**, between the wheels, are presented as his idea.

The fair wording: Gianni Lancia was the commissioner and final arbiter of the project, credited by Nixon with the idea of the pannier tanks, which Nye instead attributes to Jano. He was not the designer of the D50.

## The end

- According to Nye, for five years he let his engineers' creativity run riot, almost regardless of cost. The company was in deep financial trouble, and the family sold it to the financier Carlo Pesenti.
- After Ascari's death at Monza in 1955, Lancia announced its withdrawal from racing.
- On 26 July 1955, six of the eight D50s were handed over to Ferrari in Turin. The two accounts differ on this transfer: Nixon writes that the Italian Automobile Club decided to give the cars to Ferrari, with Fiat contributing 50 million lire over five years; Nye mentions annual sponsorship of some £30,000 given to Ferrari by Gianni Agnelli, Gianni Lancia's "friend".
