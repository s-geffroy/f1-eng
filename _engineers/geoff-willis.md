## What he did

Geoff Willis came from naval hydrodynamics{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Arriving in F1 in 1990, he was one of the first to apply computational fluid dynamics (CFD) there, alongside [Adrian Newey]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/adrian-newey.md %}), before becoming Williams's chief aerodynamicist and then technical director of BAR-Honda and Red Bull{% include src.html n="1,2,4" %}.

## Technical career

- **Before F1.** A mechanical engineer trained at Cambridge, he worked on hydrodynamics, including submarines and torpedoes, and earned a doctorate at Exeter University{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. From 1987 he spent three years designing hulls and keels for the British America's Cup challenge in San Diego, and became heavily involved in CFD{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Leyton House, 1990.** There he developed CFD tools with Newey{% include src.html n="1,2,3" %}. GrandPrix.com calls him "the first engineer to use computational fluid dynamics in F1"{% include src.html n="1" %}; Crash.net says, more cautiously, that he helped Newey introduce it into aerodynamic design{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **Williams.** He followed Newey there as a CFD specialist and did aerodynamic research with him and Eghbal Hamidy{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. When both left in 1997, he became chief aerodynamicist and worked on all the cars with chief designer Gavin Fisher{% include src.html n="1" %}. In 2002, Atlas F1 described him as the aerodynamicist of the Williams-BMWs, under technical director [Patrick Head]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/patrick-head.md %}), with Fisher handling the mechanical side{% include src.html n="6" %}.
- **BAR-Honda.** Technical director from 2002, he rebuilt the design staff and led the design of the **BAR 005**{% include src.html n="1" %}. He says he found there "the design task in its full range of challenges"{% include src.html n="2" %}. On arrival, he found the team "probably a little too inward-looking" and reorganised it; he announced for 2003 a completely new car, with nothing carried over, neither radical nor conservative, but "mainstream" (Atlas F1){% include src.html n="6" %}.
- **The interim car (2003).** Like Ferrari, BAR planned to start 2004 with an interim car: the 005 chassis with the 2004 engine, gearbox and rear suspension, for the season's opening long-haul Grands Prix (Atlas F1, June 2003){% include src.html n="7" %}.
- **The fuel tank (2005).** The FIA Court of Appeal banned BAR for two races over the design of its fuel tank{% include src.html n="8" %}. In his witness statement, Willis called it "normal Formula One design technology", with similar designs used by other teams; he had therefore not asked the FIA for clarification (Autosport){% include src.html n="8" %}. He left Honda in June 2006, a few hours after Shuhei Nakamoto was named senior technical director above him{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Red Bull, HRT.** Technical director of Red Bull Racing from 2007, he left the team in July 2009 after his job was made redundant: Newey explained that four or five department heads would be given more responsibility instead of a single technical director (Autosport){% include src.html n="4,9" %}. He became a consultant to HRT in March 2010{% include src.html n="4" %}.
- **Mercedes.** Technology director from 17 October 2011, under technical director [Bob Bell]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/bob-bell.md %}), he led aerodynamics, vehicle dynamics, control systems and simulation{% include src.html n="4" %}. [Ross Brawn]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ross-brawn.md %}) saw his division of work with [Aldo Costa]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/aldo-costa.md %}) as "natural areas of responsibility"{% include src.html n="5" %}. In January 2017, when [Paddy Lowe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/paddy-lowe.md %}) left, Mercedes still named him, as technology director, among the senior directors leading its technical organisation (Autosport){% include src.html n="10" %}.
- **The America's Cup.** In October 2021, INEOS announced that Willis, through Mercedes-AMG F1 Applied Science, would be dedicated to the British challenge INEOS Britannia as technical director; the announcement recalled his time with Peter de Savary's Blue Arrow Challenge{% include src.html n="11" %}.

## Attribution

The arrival of CFD in F1 was a shared piece of work: the sources consistently pair Willis with Newey, at Leyton House and then at Williams{% include src.html n="1,2,3" %}. This site does not credit him alone with introducing it. Beyond that, the sources consulted credit him with no specific technical solution on the Williams, Red Bull or Mercedes cars: they describe leadership roles.

## Where sources disagree

- **Time at Williams**: 1991 to 2002 according to Crash.net{% include src.html n="2" %}; about twelve years according to Atlas F1 in 2002{% include src.html n="6" %}; GrandPrix.com, in 2006, speaks of five years as head of aerodynamics{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Arrival at BAR**: late 2001 according to Crash.net{% include src.html n="2" %}, early 2002 according to GrandPrix.com{% include src.html n="1" %}.
