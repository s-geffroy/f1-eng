## What he did

Derek Gardner designed the early Tyrrells, from the 1970 001 to the 1976 **six-wheeled P34**, the only six-wheeled car ever to start a Grand Prix{% include src.html n="2,3" %}.

## Technical career

- **Transmission.** A transmission specialist, he came from Harry Ferguson, where he worked on four-wheel drive{% include src.html n="3,4" %}; he did the same at Matra in F1, in 1969{% include src.html n="2,3,4" %}.
- **Lotus.** On secondment to Lotus, he was responsible for the four-wheel-drive part of [Colin Chapman]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/colin-chapman.md %})'s Indianapolis turbine car; he supplied centre differentials that shifted the torque split towards the rear{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
- **Tyrrell 001 (1970).** He joined Ken Tyrrell in 1970 and built the first Tyrrell in his garage{% include src.html n="3" %}. Jackie Stewart put it on pole at its first race, the 1970 Canadian Grand Prix, before retiring{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Tyrrell 003 and 006.** In 1971, Stewart and François Cevert won seven races for Tyrrell; Tyrrell took the constructors' title and Stewart the drivers' title{% include src.html n="3,4" %}. In 1973, the 006 brought him his third title{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Tyrrell P34 (1976-1977).** See below.

## The P34: less lift at the front

- **The idea.** According to Gardner, it went back to 1968: faced with the reactions of four-wheel drive, he drew a car with four front wheels, two of them driven, and proposed it, without success, to Andy Granatelli, Lotus's sponsor at Indianapolis{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
- **The aim.** People assumed the point was to reduce frontal area; Gardner denied it; the rear wheels, huge though normal-sized, set that area anyway, note Nigel Roebuck and Andrew Frankel, who allows it a small part{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. According to Motor Sport, the aim was [downforce]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#appui){% include src.html n="2" %}. An exposed wheel generates [lift]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#portance), all the more so the bigger it is; four small front wheels generate less and allow the front [wing]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#aileron) to be run with less load{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Gardner put the gain at some forty horsepower{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **The tyres.** Goodyear made the small front tyres{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Gardner wanted 9-inch wheels; the compromise was 10 inches, Mini size{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **Racing.** Announced at the end of 1975, the P34 made its debut at the 1976 Spanish Grand Prix, at Jarama{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. Jody Scheckter won the Swedish Grand Prix, at Anderstorp, ahead of Patrick Depailler; they finished third and fourth in the championship{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
- **The decline.** From 1976, Gardner told Andrew Frankel, Goodyear put most of its effort behind Ferrari and McLaren; in 1977, he told Nigel Roebuck, Goodyear, which supplied the whole field, developed the other teams' tyres and neglected the small front tyres used by Tyrrell alone{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. According to Gardner, the concept's advantage evaporated{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## Where sources disagree

- **His departure.** GrandPrix.com says he was disheartened by the P34's results{% include src.html n="3" %}; OldRacingCars.com mentions ill health{% include src.html n="4" %}. Motor Sport dates it to after the 1977 Italian Grand Prix{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. He then became a researcher at Borg-Warner{% include src.html n="3,4" %}.
- **Scheckter's verdict.** The Anderstorp winner rated the car poorly ("a piece of junk", according to GrandPrix.com){% include src.html n="2,3" %}; Depailler, according to Gardner, took to it straight away{% include src.html n="1,2" %}.
