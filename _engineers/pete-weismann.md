## What he did

Pete Weismann was an American transmission specialist, a "US wizard" in Motor Sport's words. He designed several gearboxes for [Gordon Murray]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gordon-murray.md %}), from the Brabham BT55 to the 1988 McLaren MP4/4, including its vertically stacked three-shaft gearbox.

## His gearboxes

- **Brabham, 1971.** According to Motor Sport, the idea of the laid-down BT55 came from a Brabham IndyCar project Murray worked on with [Ron Tauranac]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ron-tauranac.md %}) in 1971: a lay-down Offenhauser engine and a Weismann gearbox. Autosport traces his relationship with Brabham back to the 1960s.
- **The BT55 (1986).** Murray asked him for "another modern Z-drive gearbox": "Pete came up with a Z-drive gearbox; the gears brought the output shaft back in line with the diff." Motor Sport's spec box gives a Weismann seven-speed transverse manual. Autosport calls Weismann and [David North]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/david-north.md %}) "the twin brains" behind that gearbox.
- **The MP4/4 (1988).** The Honda V6's crankshaft was 28mm lower; a two-shaft gearbox would have meant power-sapping driveshaft angles. "I decided we needed a bit of genius and phoned Pete Weisman, who came up with an amazing three-shaft gearbox," Murray recalls. Weismann also offered him a completely dry-sump gearbox, with the transfer gears at the front under a close-fitting magnesium cover. Murray describes it as the first true dry-sump gearbox: very noisy, with no oil to damp the vibrations, but reliable.

## Attribution

According to Autosport, the MP4/4 gearbox was Murray's project, "his baby", and nobody disputes it. Weismann and North designed it: Murray says Weismann "came to the rescue with David".

## Source discrepancies

- **The MP4/4 gearbox**: Autosport credits Weismann and North together; in Motor Sport in 2005, Murray names only Weismann.
- **The BT55 layout**: according to Keith Howard, Weismann had suggested the car's layout; his widow, Michelle, blamed its failure on a lack of effort from BMW. Murray presents the laid-down car as his own idea, drawn from the 1971 project.
- **The Z-drive**: Autosport writes that for the MP4/4 Murray turned to the authors of the Z-drive gearbox; Murray said in 2026 that he knew he would not have a Z-drive gearbox at McLaren.
- **Spelling**: "Weismann" in Autosport and in Motor Sport's BT55 spec box; "Weisman" in the 2005 article.

## A short entry

Weismann, who had died by 2005 according to Motor Sport, which quotes his widow, and whom Autosport calls "the late", is known through Murray's recollections. This site knows neither his dates nor the history of his company, and sticks to these facts.
