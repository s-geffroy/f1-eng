## What he did

Pete Weismann was an American transmission specialist, a "US wizard" in Motor Sport's words{% include src.html n="2,3" %}. He designed several gearboxes for [Gordon Murray]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/gordon-murray.md %}), from the Brabham BT49 to the 1988 McLaren MP4/4, including its vertically stacked three-shaft gearbox, and then the McLaren F1 road car's{% include src.html n="1,2,4,10" %}.

His company, Traction Products, in Costa Mesa, California, was founded in 1963 by Peter and Albert Weismann around a limited-slip differential, the "Weismann Locker", according to the company's website{% include src.html n="11" %}. Motor Sport, which quotes his widow in 2005, and Autosport, which calls him "the late" in 2017, report him dead{% include src.html n="1,2" %}; this site does not know his dates.

## His gearboxes

- **Brabham, 1971.** According to Motor Sport, the idea of the laid-down BT55 came from a Brabham IndyCar project led by Murray with [Ron Tauranac]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/ron-tauranac.md %}) in 1971: a laid-down Offenhauser engine and a Weismann gearbox{% include src.html n="3" %}. For that project, "Pete came up with a Z-drive gearbox; the gears brought the output shaft back in line with the diff," Murray recalls{% include src.html n="3" %}. Autosport traces his relationship with Brabham back to the 1960s{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- **The BT49 (1980).** In May 1980, Denis Jenkinson (Motor Sport) wrote that Brabham had been working with Weismann for some while on a new gearbox/final-drive unit for the BT49: it was his first entry into F1, after USAC and Can-Am, but many teams already used his limited-slip differential{% include src.html n="4" %}. The shafts ran [transversely]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#boite-transversale), beneath the differential; nothing stood behind the housing, which improved the [polar moment of inertia]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#moment-polaire) and let the spring/damper units sit out of the airstream{% include src.html n="4" %}. The casing bore the name "MRD – Weismann": it was a joint project with Brabham{% include src.html n="4" %}. Tried at Long Beach on BT49/03, the gearbox was not considered race-worthy{% include src.html n="4" %}. At Imola, in September, the Brabhams had gone back to Hewland gearboxes; Weismann was there, but none of his gearboxes{% include src.html n="5" %}. Rick Mears recalls a Weismann gearbox test at Riverside the same year{% include src.html n="6" %}.
- **The BT55 (1986).** Murray asked him for "another modern Z-drive gearbox", modelled on the 1971 one{% include src.html n="3" %}. Motor Sport's spec box gives a Weismann seven-speed transverse manual{% include src.html n="3" %}. Autosport calls Weismann and [David North]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/david-north.md %}) "the twin brains" behind that gearbox{% include src.html n="1" %}. In 1986, Jenkinson described it as explained by Weismann, its "designer and builder": seven straight-cut speeds on transverse shafts, in effect "a big strong motorcycle type gearbox", with double-sided dog rings and less than a quarter of an inch of selector movement between gears{% include src.html n="7" %}.
- **The MP4/4 (1988).** The Honda [V6]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#architecture-moteur)'s crankshaft was 28mm lower; a two-shaft gearbox would have meant power-sapping driveshaft angles{% include src.html n="1" %}. "I decided we needed a bit of genius and phoned Pete Weisman [sic], who came up with an amazing three-shaft gearbox," Murray recalls{% include src.html n="2" %}. Weismann also offered him a completely dry-sump gearbox, with the transfer gears at the front under a close-fitting magnesium cover{% include src.html n="2" %}. Murray describes it as the first true dry-sump gearbox: very noisy, with no oil to damp the vibrations, but reliable{% include src.html n="2" %}.
- **The McLaren F1 (1992).** For the road car designed by Murray, McLaren and BMW turned to Traction Products, run by Weismann and his wife{% include src.html n="10" %}. The six-speed transverse gearbox combined Weismann's design with synchronisers from a BMW 5 Series gearbox; it was built in England by FF Developments (Road & Track, 2018){% include src.html n="10" %}.

## Attribution

According to Autosport, the MP4/4 gearbox was Murray's project, "his baby", and nobody disputes it{% include src.html n="1" %}. Weismann and North designed it: Murray says Weismann "came to the rescue with David"{% include src.html n="1,9" %}.

## Where sources disagree

- **The MP4/4 gearbox**: Autosport (2017) and Motor Sport (2024) credit Weismann and North together{% include src.html n="1,9" %}; in Motor Sport in 2005, Murray names only Weismann{% include src.html n="2" %}. [Steve Nichols]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/steve-nichols.md %}), for his part, explained in 2021 that Weismann was brought in to oversee the gearbox (Motor Sport){% include src.html n="8" %}.
- **The BT55 layout**: according to Keith Howard, Weismann had suggested the car's layout; his widow, Michelle, blamed its failure on a lack of effort from BMW{% include src.html n="2" %}. Murray presents the laid-down car as his own idea, drawn from the 1971 project{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **The Z-drive**: Autosport writes that for the MP4/4 Murray turned to the authors of the Z-drive gearbox{% include src.html n="1" %}; Murray said in 2026 that he knew he would not have a Z-drive gearbox at McLaren{% include src.html n="3" %}.
- **Spelling**: the 2005 Motor Sport article, the source of Murray's quote, writes "Weisman", whereas Autosport and Motor Sport's BT55 spec box write "Weismann"{% include src.html n="1,2,3" %}.
