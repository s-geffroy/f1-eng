## What he did

Alan Styman was Lotus's chief draughtsman at the time of the **Lotus 25** (1962), the first [monocoque]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#monocoque) F1 car to succeed{% include src.html n="1,2" %}. [Colin Chapman]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/colin-chapman.md %}) did its overall layout and exterior shape; according to Motor Sport, much of the detail work was carried out by Styman and Ian Jones{% include src.html n="1" %}.

## On the Lotus 25

- He described his job himself: to interpret Chapman's layout and compile detail drawings for manufacture{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- According to him, the first chassis was mainly made to their drawings, but many parts were made in the workshop and, if necessary, drawn afterwards{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- The suspension parts were virtually carried over from the Lotus 24, its [spaceframe]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#chassis-tubulaire) sister, with new pick-ups on the tub{% include src.html n="1" %}.
- Doug Nye writes that the first chassis, "R1", drawn by Styman, was fabricated in secret{% include src.html n="2" %}.

## Limits of the sources

Styman is known only through what articles on the Lotus 25 or on Chapman say about the car. The Colin Chapman Museum puts him first in its list of the people "believed" to have made up the team Chapman assembled for the 25, but without giving roles{% include src.html n="3" %}. This site sticks to those facts.
