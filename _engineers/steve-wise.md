## What he did

Steve Wise is the engineer behind the **electronic hardware** of Williams [active suspension]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#suspension-active). In late 1987, he joined Williams on the same day as [Paddy Lowe]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/paddy-lowe.md %}), with the shared task of creating an electronics department.

- The roles were divided: hardware for Wise, software for Lowe. [Frank Dernie]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/frank-dernie.md %}) recalls finding "two young guys", one for the hardware, the other for the programming. According to Lowe, Dernie was at first looking for a technician, and realised on interviewing them that he needed engineers: Williams then had no internal capability for building electronic control systems.
- According to Lowe, over three or four years the two men created a computer "fit for purpose", with the harnesses, instrumentation, control valves and software. Lowe was project leader of active suspension; Wise, project manager of the [semi-automatic gearbox]({{ site.baseurl }}/en/glossary/#boite-semi-automatique) control.
- For the 1990 test programme, he designed a new Williams control unit and a new data logger, with more advanced processors. The Race (2021) specifies that he designed the original Vehicle Control Module (VCM), the first-generation computer control system used for the active ride programme, tested in 1990 by Mark Blundell; Chris Saunders, who shared their office, describes Lowe and Wise as the heads of electronics.
- [Patrick Head]({{ site.baseurl }}{% link _engineers/patrick-head.md %}) names him among the engineers responsible for the various active suspension systems and their development.
- He later became head of electronics at Williams. On the 1993 FW15C, writes Adam Cooper (Motor Sport, 2023), much of the electronics was developed by just two engineers, Lowe and Wise.
- In 2016, Motor Sport's article on Lowe described him as still with Williams.

## A short entry

Steve Wise only appears in articles devoted to others (Dernie, Lowe, Head, the FW15C, Newey's arrival at Williams); no interview with him has been found. Nor do the sources say exactly which of Wise and Lowe designed the first computer: The Race credits Wise, Lowe speaks of joint work. This site sticks to those mentions.
